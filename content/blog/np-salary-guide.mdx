**Quick answer:** The U.S. median annual wage for nurse practitioners is $129,210 (BLS OEWS, Nurse Practitioners 29-1171, May 2024). Around that median sits a wide spread, and five things explain most of it: state, practice setting, specialty, employment structure, and practice authority. They move the number more than years of experience alone. For a specific state, setting, or specialty, the honest figure is the median of live postings with disclosed pay, which is what the salary guide on this board reports once enough employers disclose it.

## The national picture

The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median annual wage for nurse practitioners at $129,210 (BLS OEWS, May 2024). Just as important as the level is the demand behind it: BLS Employment Projections put nurse practitioner employment growth at about 40% from 2024 to 2034. Employers competing for a scarce clinician pool compete on compensation first.

A median is a midpoint, not a quote, and the spread around it is wide. Actual offers depend on where you practice, what setting you work in, and how the role is structured.

## The five factors that move NP pay

**1. State.** Pay differs substantially by state, and higher-paying states are often more expensive places to live. A smaller number in a lower-cost state can out-earn a bigger number in a coastal metro once housing is priced in. The [salary guide](/salary-guide) on this board publishes a median for each state once enough postings with disclosed pay from enough distinct employers support it, and says so plainly when they do not.

**2. Practice setting.** Inpatient, emergency, and acute-care roles tend to price above outpatient primary care, reflecting acuity, shift patterns, and call. Shift differentials, weekend premiums, and on-call stipends stack on top of base salary in hospital settings.

**3. Specialty and certification.** Certified registered nurse anesthetists sit at the top of the APRN pay scale, and high-demand prescriber specialties also command premiums. Neither premium is a fixed number: it shows up in the postings for a given state and setting, which is why our comparison of the [highest-paying NP specialties](/blog/highest-paying-np-specialties) explains the structure behind each premium rather than quoting a band.

**4. Employment structure.** A full-time W-2 role bundles health insurance, a retirement match, a CME allowance, malpractice coverage, and paid time off into the package. Independent-contractor (1099) roles quote a higher headline rate but shift taxes, malpractice, and benefits onto you, so the hourly figure is not take-home pay. Our [1099 vs W-2 breakdown](/blog/np-1099-vs-w2) walks through the math.

**5. Practice authority.** In the 27 states + DC that grant full practice authority (AANP State Practice Environment, 2025), NPs can practice and prescribe without a required physician relationship, in some states after a transition-to-practice period. Full practice authority widens your options (independent practice, telehealth panels, and practice ownership), and options are negotiating leverage.

## How to use salary data in a negotiation

Anchor on evidence, not instinct. Bring the BLS national median, the live median for your state and setting where this board publishes one, and two or three comparable open roles. Then negotiate the whole package: base, productivity or quality bonuses, CME budget, licensure and certification reimbursement, PTO, and call expectations. A small base difference can be dwarfed by a weaker retirement match or an extra weekend rotation.

If you are comparing a W-2 offer against contract or per-diem rates, net out the benefits the contractor self-funds before concluding that the hourly rate "pays more."

### What is the median nurse practitioner salary?

### Which nurse practitioners get paid the most?

Certified registered nurse anesthetists (CRNAs) sit at the top of the APRN pay scale. Among NP roles, inpatient, emergency, and acute-care settings generally price above outpatient primary care.

### What is the job outlook for nurse practitioners?

BLS Employment Projections put nurse practitioner employment growth at about 40% from 2024 to 2034. Sustained demand is the quiet force behind NP pay, because employers competing for a scarce clinician pool compete on compensation first.

### Do nurse practitioners earn more in full practice authority states?

Full practice authority does not guarantee a higher base salary, but the 27 states + DC with FPA (AANP, 2025) give NPs more earning paths (independent practice, telehealth panels, and practice ownership), which strengthens their negotiating position.

### How accurate are salary ranges in job postings?

Treat posted ranges as an opening position. Cross-check them against the live state median in our salary guide, which is computed from current postings with disclosed pay, and against the BLS national median.
