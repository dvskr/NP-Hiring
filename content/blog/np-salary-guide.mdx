**Quick answer:** Two nurse practitioner offers for similar work can differ widely because of five things: the state, the practice setting, the specialty, the employment structure, and the state's practice authority rules. This article explains how each one moves pay and how to spot it in a job posting. For the figures themselves, use the [NP salary guide](/salary-guide): it reports the national median, and a state median wherever enough postings with disclosed pay from enough employers support one.

---

## Why one national median is not your number

The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median annual wage for nurse practitioners at $132,300 (BLS OEWS, May 2025), and BLS Employment Projections put nurse practitioner employment growth at about 40% from 2024 to 2034. Both are national figures. A median is a midpoint across every state and setting, not a quote, and real offers sit above and below it. The [salary guide](/salary-guide) tracks the national figure and publishes a state median wherever enough postings with disclosed pay from enough employers support one. The five factors below explain the spread.

## 1. State

Pay differs by state, and so does what a salary buys. A bigger figure in an expensive metro can leave you with less than a smaller one elsewhere once housing and state taxes are counted, so compare offers on what they leave you, not on the headline. State also sets your licensure lead time: moving to a state outside the Nurse Licensure Compact adds an RN endorsement step before your APRN application, which can push back your start date and your first paycheck. The [scope of practice explorer](/scope-of-practice) shows each state's compact status and practice rule.

## 2. Practice setting

Setting changes how pay is built as much as its level. Inpatient, emergency and acute care roles often add shift differentials, night and weekend premiums and on-call stipends to the base, reflecting acuity, shift patterns and call, and those only show up if you ask for the full compensation plan. Outpatient roles more often add a productivity component instead, which is worth something only if its threshold is reachable.

## 3. Specialty and certification

In BLS wage data, certified registered nurse anesthetists have the highest median pay of the APRN roles, and specialties where employers compete for prescribers can carry a premium. A premium is not a fixed number. It shows up in the postings for a given state and setting, and it depends on how scarce your certification is in that market. Our comparison of the [highest-paying NP specialties](/blog/highest-paying-np-specialties) explains the structure behind each premium, and the [certification overview](/blog/np-certification-overview) covers which body certifies each track.

## 4. Employment structure

A full-time W-2 role bundles health insurance, a retirement match, a CME allowance, malpractice coverage and paid time off into the package. Independent contractor (1099) roles usually quote a higher headline rate but shift taxes, malpractice and benefits onto you, so the hourly figure is not take-home pay. Per diem roles usually trade guaranteed hours for flexibility, and they can be structured either way. The [1099 vs W-2 comparison](/resources/1099-vs-w2) prices the difference with worked numbers, and if you are weighing a contract, the [1099 contract guide](/blog/np-1099-vs-w2) covers the terms to negotiate.

## 5. Practice authority

AANP classifies 27 states + DC as full practice (AANP State Practice Environment, 2026). There, state law lets NPs evaluate, diagnose, treat and prescribe under the licensure authority of the board of nursing, although several of those states first require a transition period of collaborative or supervised practice. Practice authority does not set a salary. It sets which ways of earning are open to you: independent practice, telehealth panels across states, and contract work without a collaborator to arrange and pay. In reduced and restricted practice states, ask who arranges and funds any collaborating physician, because a fee charged back to you is a pay cut. The [full practice authority guide](/resources/fpa-guide) explains what the classification changes.

## How to read these factors in a job posting

- **Check the pay basis.** Hourly, per visit, salary, or base plus productivity. Convert every offer to the same basis before you compare, and count only guaranteed hours.
- **Separate base from total compensation.** Differentials, call pay, bonuses and sign-on money belong in the comparison, but only the parts that are written down.
- **Price the benefits.** Health insurance, a retirement match, a CME allowance, malpractice and paid time off can outweigh a difference in base pay.
- **Note the structure.** A 1099 rate and a W-2 salary are not the same kind of number.
- **Read the practice rule.** In a state that requires an agreement, find out who provides it and who pays for it.

Then compare the offer with the state median in the salary guide where one is published, and with two or three comparable open roles on [the jobs board](/jobs). When you are ready to counter, the [salary negotiation guide](/blog/np-salary-negotiation-guide) has scripts for the base and for everything around it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What affects nurse practitioner pay?

The main factors are the state, the practice setting, the specialty and certification, the employment structure, and the state's practice authority rules. The [salary guide](/salary-guide) reports the national median and the state medians this board can support from live postings.

### Do nurse practitioners earn more in full practice authority states?

Full practice authority, which AANP assigns to 27 states + DC (AANP, 2026), does not guarantee a higher base salary. It widens the ways you can earn, such as independent practice, telehealth panels and contract work without a collaborator to arrange, and that strengthens your negotiating position.

### Does a higher 1099 rate mean higher pay?

Not necessarily. A 1099 rate is quoted before self-employment tax, malpractice, health insurance, retirement and unpaid time off, all of which the contractor funds. Compare both structures on the same basis before deciding which one pays more.

### How accurate are salary ranges in job postings?

Treat a posted range as an opening position. Check it against the state median in the salary guide where one is published, against the BLS national median, and against comparable open roles in the same setting.

### Does experience change NP pay?

Yes, mostly through the same five factors. Experience opens higher acuity settings and specialty roles, in some states it ends a transition period, and it strengthens your hand in a negotiation. Ask how an employer's pay scale credits your prior NP and RN experience.
