**Quick answer:** CRNAs (certified registered nurse anesthetists) top the APRN pay scale, with postings typically at $180K–$250K+. Below them, acuity and scarcity set the order: inpatient, emergency, and acute-care NP roles generally price above the typical $110K–$170K full-time band, while outpatient primary care anchors it. The all-NP national median is $129,210 (BLS OEWS, May 2024).

---

## What actually creates a specialty premium

Specialty pay differences aren't arbitrary — they track four structural factors:

1. **Training scarcity.** The longer and more selective the pipeline, the higher the floor. CRNA programs are the clearest example.
2. **Acuity and liability.** Managing unstable patients — anesthesia, ICU, emergency — commands more than managing stable ones.
3. **Schedule burden.** Nights, weekends, holidays, and call carry differentials that compound over a year.
4. **Prescriber scarcity.** Where demand for prescribing clinicians outruns supply, compensation follows.

Keep those in mind and the specialty rankings below stop being trivia and start being levers you can pull.

## The tiers, from the top

**CRNA — the ceiling.** Nurse anesthesia carries the highest APRN pay bands: postings typically run $180K–$250K+, with compensation varying by call burden and whether the practice runs a supervision-based or independent CRNA model. Certification runs through NBCRNA, and the doctoral-level training pipeline is the price of entry. Browse [anesthesia roles](/jobs/anesthesia) to see current structures.

**Acute care, hospitalist, and emergency — the acuity premium.** [Acute care](/jobs/acute-care), [hospitalist](/jobs/hospitalist), and [emergency](/jobs/emergency) NP roles concentrate in ICUs, step-down units, and EDs, typically on shift patterns where night and weekend differentials plus on-call stipends stack on base salary. Employers generally expect AGACNP or ENP preparation and prior high-acuity experience.

**Behavioral health — the scarcity premium.** Prescriber shortages keep demand elevated nationwide; behavioral-health NP roles typically post at $120K–$170K, and the specialty's telehealth-friendliness widens the market further.

**Primary care and outpatient — the volume anchor.** [Family practice](/jobs/family-practice) and [outpatient](/jobs/outpatient) roles anchor the typical $110K–$170K full-time band. The premium path here isn't the specialty itself — it's ownership and independent practice, especially in the 27 states + DC with full practice authority (AANP, 2025).

## Structure can beat specialty

Two caveats before you re-enroll in a certificate program:

**Employment structure moves pay as much as specialty.** Travel assignments typically carry 20–50% premiums over permanent equivalents; independent-contractor (1099) work posts at $60–$150+ per hour but shifts taxes, malpractice, and benefits onto you — see our [1099 vs W-2 explainer](/blog/np-1099-vs-w2).

**Geography compounds everything.** A high-paying specialty in a low-paying state can net less than a modest specialty in a strong market. Cross-reference any specialty move against live state data in the [salary guide](/salary-guide).

## Negotiating within your specialty

Whatever tier you practice in, the levers inside an offer are the same. Differentials and call stipends are often more negotiable than base salary, especially in hospital settings where the base is banded. CME budget, licensure and certification reimbursement, and retirement match move real money without touching the salary line. And in any specialty with a scarcity premium, a competing offer — even one you don't intend to take — is the strongest evidence you can bring to the table. Anchor every conversation on live posting data for your state and setting rather than a national average.

## Choosing with eyes open

Chasing the highest band only makes sense if the work suits you — burnout in a premium specialty costs more than the premium pays. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the highest-paid NP specialty?

Nurse anesthesia. CRNA postings typically run $180K–$250K+, the highest APRN band — reflecting the selective doctoral training pipeline (certification through NBCRNA) and the acuity of the work.

### How much more do acute care NPs make than primary care NPs?

There's no fixed gap, but inpatient, emergency, and acute-care roles generally price above outpatient primary care within and beyond the typical $110K–$170K band, with shift differentials and call stipends adding to base pay.

### Why do behavioral health NPs earn a premium?

Prescriber supply hasn't kept pace with demand, so employers compete for the available clinicians. Behavioral-health NP roles typically post at $120K–$170K, and telehealth demand adds further pressure.

### Can a primary care NP out-earn a specialty NP?

Yes — through structure and geography. Practice ownership in a full-practice-authority state, travel contracts at 20–50% premiums, or high-paying-state markets can each outweigh a specialty premium.

### Do I need a new certification to switch specialties?

Often, yes — population-focus certifications (for example acute care vs family) are distinct credentials with their own programs and exams through bodies like AANP, ANCC, NBCRNA, and AMCB. Verify what your target state's board requires before investing.
