**Quick answer:** CRNAs (certified registered nurse anesthetists) top the APRN pay scale. Below them, acuity and scarcity set the order: inpatient, emergency, and acute-care NP roles generally price above outpatient primary care, which anchors the NP market. The all-NP national median is $132,300 (BLS OEWS, May 2025). Every premium above that anchor is a structure you can read in live postings for your state and setting, not a fixed band.

---

## What creates a specialty premium

Specialty pay differences are not arbitrary. They track four structural factors:

1. **Training scarcity.** The longer and more selective the pipeline, the higher the floor. CRNA programs are the clearest example.
2. **Acuity and liability.** Managing unstable patients (anesthesia, ICU, emergency) commands more than managing stable ones.
3. **Schedule burden.** Nights, weekends, holidays, and call carry differentials that compound over a year.
4. **Prescriber scarcity.** Where demand for prescribing clinicians outruns supply, compensation follows.

## The tiers, from the top

**CRNA: the ceiling.** Nurse anesthesia carries the highest APRN pay, with compensation varying by call burden and by whether the practice runs a supervision-based or independent CRNA model. The pipeline explains much of it: every US nurse anesthesia program is now at the doctoral level, with a minimum length of 36 months, and graduates must pass the National Certification Examination administered by NBCRNA ([COA](https://www.coacrna.org/about-coa/requirements-to-practice-as-a-nurse-anesthetist-in-the-united-states/)). The [nurse anesthetist salary page](/salary-guide/specialty/anesthesia) shows the BLS figure for the occupation and the settings where CRNAs work.

**Acute care, hospitalist, and emergency: the acuity premium.** [Acute care](/jobs/acute-care), [hospitalist](/jobs/hospitalist), and [emergency](/jobs/emergency) NP roles concentrate in ICUs, step-down units, and EDs, typically on shift patterns where night and weekend differentials and on-call stipends stack on top of base salary. Employers generally expect acute care certification, such as AGACNP-BC from ANCC or ACNPC-AG from AACN, or emergency preparation such as the ENP exam from AANPCB (operating as NPCB), plus prior high-acuity experience.

**Behavioral health: the scarcity premium.** Where psychiatric prescribers are scarce, employers compete for the clinicians available, so [psychiatric mental health](/jobs/psychiatric-mental-health) NP roles carry a scarcity premium in many markets. The specialty's suitability for telehealth widens the market further.

**Primary care and outpatient: the volume anchor.** [Family practice](/jobs/family-practice) roles anchor the NP market. The premium path here is not the specialty itself. It is ownership and independent practice, especially in the 27 states + DC with [full practice authority](https://www.aanp.org/advocacy/state/state-practice-environment) (AANP, 2026). Many full practice states still require transition-to-practice hours first: Minnesota, for example, requires at least 2,080 hours within a collaborative agreement before independent practice ([Minn. Stat. § 148.211](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/148.211)). Check your state's rules on the [scope of practice reference](/scope-of-practice).

## Why the NP median leaves out CRNAs

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reports nurse anesthetists, nurse midwives, and nurse practitioners as three separate occupations, each with its own median wage ([BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm)). The $132,300 figure above covers nurse practitioners only. A figure that blends CRNA pay into "NP salary" overstates what most NPs earn, so compare each role against its own occupation.

## How setting and schedule premiums show up in postings

A premium usually shows up in how the pay is structured:

1. **Separate differentials.** Hospital postings often list a base rate plus night, weekend, or holiday differentials. Ask whether the posted figure includes them.
2. **Call pay.** On-call pay may be a flat stipend, an hourly rate, or folded into salary, and each produces a different annual total.
3. **Hourly against annual.** Per diem, locum, and contract rates usually exclude benefits, so compare them with salaried roles only once you know the scheduled hours.

## Structure can beat specialty

Employment structure moves pay as much as specialty does. Travel assignments typically carry a premium in exchange for short terms and relocation. Independent-contractor (1099) work quotes a higher hourly headline but shifts taxes, malpractice, and benefits onto you; see our [1099 vs W-2 comparison](/resources/1099-vs-w2). Geography compounds everything: a high-paying specialty in a low-paying state can net less than a modest specialty in a strong market.

## Negotiating and choosing

Differentials and call stipends are often more negotiable than base salary, especially where the hospital base is banded. CME budget, certification reimbursement, and retirement match move real money without touching the salary line. Chasing the highest band only makes sense if the work suits you; our comparison of [FNP vs PMHNP vs AGACNP](/blog/fnp-vs-pmhnp-vs-agacnp) walks through the day-to-day differences behind the pay.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the highest-paid NP specialty?

Nurse anesthesia sits at the top of the APRN pay scale, though CRNAs are a separate APRN role rather than an NP specialty. The premium reflects a doctoral training pipeline, certification through NBCRNA, and the acuity of the work. Posted figures vary by state and practice model, so read them from live postings rather than a national band.

### How much more do acute care NPs make than primary care NPs?

There is no fixed gap. Inpatient, emergency, and acute-care roles generally price above outpatient primary care, and shift differentials and call stipends add to base pay. The real difference depends on your state, the setting, and how many nights and call shifts the schedule requires.

### Is a CRNA a nurse practitioner?

No. Both are advanced practice registered nurses, but BLS counts nurse anesthetists and nurse practitioners as separate occupations with separate pay data. CRNAs train in doctoral nurse anesthesia programs and certify through NBCRNA, while NPs certify in a population focus such as family, acute care, or psychiatric mental health.

### Why do behavioral health NPs earn a premium?

Where psychiatric prescribers are scarce, employers compete for the clinicians available, and telehealth lets them recruit across a wider area. The premium is local rather than universal, so compare postings in your own state and setting.

### Can a primary care NP out-earn a specialty NP?

Yes, through structure and geography. Practice ownership in a full practice authority state, travel contracts, or a high-paying state market can each outweigh a specialty premium. Confirm your state's transition-to-practice rules before planning independent practice.

### Do I need a new certification to switch specialties?

Often, yes. Population-focus certifications (for example, acute care versus family) are distinct credentials with their own programs and exams through bodies such as AANPCB (NPCB), ANCC, AACN, NBCRNA, and AMCB. Verify what your target state's board requires before investing.
