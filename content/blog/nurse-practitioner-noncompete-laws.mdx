**Quick answer:** As of October 2026, whether a nurse practitioner non compete clause is enforceable depends on state law and the contract's wording. The FTC's nationwide ban never took effect and was removed from federal regulations on February 12, 2026. Several states now void or limit noncompetes for NPs, including Virginia (from July 1, 2026), Maine, Colorado, Montana, Minnesota and California, while Texas allows them only under strict conditions.

---

This guide covers federal law, the state statutes that reach NPs and other advanced practice registered nurses (APRNs), and the contract terms to check before you sign. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Are noncompete agreements enforceable for nurse practitioners?

Sometimes, and the answer turns on the state whose law governs the contract. A noncompete (also called a covenant not to compete) is a contract term that bars you from practicing within a set area, for a set time, after you leave an employer.

In some states a noncompete against an NP is void outright. In others it is allowed if it meets conditions, such as a buyout option, a short duration or a small radius. Elsewhere, enforceability depends on that state's general contract and noncompete law, so identical contracts can fare differently in two states.

Three questions decide most cases: which state's law applies (look for a choice of law clause), when the contract was signed or renewed (every state law in the table below except California's applies only to agreements made, extended or renewed on or after its effective date), and whether you fall into an exception, such as owning part of the practice.

## Did the FTC ban noncompete agreements?

No. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) issued a rule in 2024 that would have banned most noncompetes, but it never took effect, and it no longer exists. Here is the dated sequence, from the FTC and the Federal Register:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| August 20, 2024 | A federal district court in Texas issued an order stopping the FTC from enforcing the rule ([FTC: Noncompete Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule)), in *Ryan, LLC v. FTC*. |
| September 5, 2025 | The FTC took steps to dismiss its appeal in the Fifth Circuit ([FTC: Noncompete Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule)). |
| September 10, 2025 | FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson sent letters to several large healthcare employers and staffing firms urging a comprehensive review of their noncompetes and other restrictive agreements ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-chairman-ferguson-issues-noncompete-warning-letters-healthcare-employers-staffing-companies)). |
| February 12, 2026 | A final rule in the Federal Register removed the Non-Compete Rule (16 CFR part 910) from the Code of Federal Regulations, effective that day ([91 FR 6507](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-12/pdf/2026-02866.pdf)). |

The FTC has not left the field. Its September 2025 release pointed to noncompetes that may unreasonably limit options "for vital roles like nurses, physicians, and other medical professionals," and said it has authority under Section 5 of the FTC Act to investigate noncompetes that are "unjustified, overbroad, or otherwise unfair" ([FTC, September 10, 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-chairman-ferguson-issues-noncompete-warning-letters-healthcare-employers-staffing-companies)). That is a power to investigate particular employers, not a rule that voids your clause, so state law remains the controlling question.

## What other contract clauses can still apply?

A noncompete ban usually leaves the rest of your contract standing. Minnesota's statute says so directly: voiding a noncompete does not void the other provisions of the agreement ([Minn. Stat. 181.988](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.988)). Expect these terms to survive in some form:

- **Nonsolicitation.** A promise not to solicit the employer's patients, clients or staff. Virginia permits a narrow version covering customers you had material contact with. Montana, by contrast, bars contracts from restricting a covered nurse from soliciting or treating current patients.
- **Confidentiality.** Nondisclosure terms protecting patient information, trade secrets and business data. Minnesota excludes these from its definition of a noncompete.
- **Repayment of sign-on, relocation or training money.** Virginia allows repayment of recruitment costs such as relocation, bonuses and training when a health care professional leaves within five years of starting.
- **Notice periods.** How many days of notice you owe before leaving, and whether the employer owes you the same.
- **Patient notice and choice.** Colorado and Virginia protect telling former patients that you continue to practice and how to reach you, and Maine requires enforceable health care noncompetes to recognize a patient's right to choose a practitioner.

## Red flags in a nurse practitioner contract

A restrictive clause is a red flag when it is broader than the employer's legitimate interest or when its trigger is one sided. Watch for these in any offer, W-2 or 1099:

1. **The noncompete applies however the job ends**, including if you are let go without cause.
2. **The radius is measured from every affiliated location**, not from where you actually practiced. A health system with many sites can turn a modest radius into a whole region.
3. **The restricted activity is "nursing" or "health care" in general**, rather than the specialty or setting you actually worked in.
4. **There is no buyout**, or the buyout is an undefined damages figure instead of a stated amount.
5. **A choice of law clause points to another state.** Minnesota, for example, bars requiring an employee who primarily lives and works there to give up Minnesota law as a condition of employment ([Minn. Stat. 181.988](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.988)); not every state has that protection.
6. **Repayment terms that never decline over time**, so leaving after three years costs as much as leaving after three months.
7. **Notice periods that bind only you**, while the employer can end the contract on short notice.
8. **Restrictions that outlast a short contract**, such as a two-year noncompete on a one-year locum assignment.

## What to negotiate in a nurse practitioner noncompete

A restrictive covenant is negotiable, like pay and schedule. Ask for the clause in writing early, then work through these levers:

- **Radius.** Tie it to the one site where you practice, measured in road miles or a stated straight line, and keep it small. Texas, for reference, caps covered noncompetes at a five-mile radius ([SB 1318](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB01318F.htm)).
- **Duration.** Shorter is better. Texas caps covered noncompetes at one year after the job ends.
- **Triggers.** Ask that the clause fall away if you are terminated without cause, if the employer does not renew, or if the employer breaches the contract.
- **Scope.** Limit it to your specialty and setting, so a psychiatric NP is not barred from unrelated primary care work.
- **Telehealth carve-out.** Ask for written language that remote care for patients outside the radius, or for a different employer's patients, is not restricted.
- **Buyout.** A stated, reasonable buyout amount turns an open-ended risk into a known price.
- **Per diem and moonlighting.** If you plan to pick up [locum tenens](/jobs/locum-tenens) or [telehealth](/jobs/telehealth) shifts, get written permission now.

## What this means for your job search and for employers

For NPs, read the restrictive covenant before you commit. A state that voids noncompetes gives you more freedom to leave later, but only for agreements within the statute's dates and terms. When you search [current NP jobs](/jobs), note the state and ask for the full agreement before the offer call. Set up [job alerts](/job-alerts) so you can compare offers rather than accept the first one under pressure.

For employers, several states changed their rules in 2025 and 2026. Virginia's statute reaches threatening to enforce a noncompete, not only enforcing one, and provides a civil penalty of $10,000 for each violation ([2026 Acts ch. 1114](https://lis.blob.core.windows.net/files/1225699.PDF)). Review templates state by state with counsel. Our [employer resources](/for-employers/resources) cover hiring practices for NP roles.

Before signing anything, have an employment attorney licensed in the state that governs your contract review it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are noncompete agreements enforceable for nurse practitioners?

It depends on the state. Some states void noncompetes for NPs, including Virginia (for agreements made or renewed from July 1, 2026), Montana, Minnesota and California. Maine bars them for health care practitioners who do not own part of their employer, and Texas allows them only with a buyout, a one-year limit and a five-mile radius. In other states, enforceability depends on that state's general noncompete law. An employment attorney in your state can tell you how it applies to your contract.

### Did the FTC ban noncompete agreements?

No. The FTC issued a nationwide ban in 2024, but a federal court stopped it on August 20, 2024. The FTC moved to dismiss its appeal on September 5, 2025, and a Federal Register rule removed the regulation effective February 12, 2026. The FTC says it can still investigate specific noncompetes under Section 5 of the FTC Act, and on September 10, 2025 it sent warning letters to several large healthcare employers and staffing firms.

### Which states restrict noncompetes for nurse practitioners?

As of October 6, 2026, statutes reaching NPs or APRNs include Virginia (effective July 1, 2026), Maine (July 29, 2026), Iowa for University of Iowa Hospitals and Clinics only (June 2, 2026), Colorado (August 6, 2025), Texas with conditions (September 1, 2025), Montana, and the general bans in Minnesota and California. This list is not exhaustive, and every law has exceptions and effective date limits.

### What is a reasonable noncompete radius for an NP?

There is no single legal answer outside states that set one. Texas caps covered health care noncompetes at a five-mile radius and one year. Elsewhere, what counts as reasonable depends on that state's law and the facts of your job. In negotiation, aim for a small radius measured from the one site where you practice, not from every affiliated location.

### Can a noncompete stop me from doing telehealth?

It can if its wording reaches remote care, for example by restricting where patients are located or barring any work for a competitor. Before signing, ask for a written carve-out for remote work outside the radius. If you already signed, have an attorney in your state read the exact language before you take a telehealth role.

### Should a lawyer review my NP employment contract?

Yes. A noncompete is only one clause, and repayment, notice, nonsolicitation, malpractice tail and termination terms can cost as much. State law decides what is enforceable, and statutes carry exceptions and effective dates that a general article cannot apply to your facts. An employment attorney licensed in the state that governs your contract can review it before you sign.
