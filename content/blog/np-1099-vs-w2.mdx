**Quick answer:** 1099 NP work quotes a higher hourly headline than a full-time W-2 salary, but the headline overstates the gap. As a 1099 contractor, you self-fund both halves of payroll tax, your own malpractice coverage, health insurance, retirement, PTO, and CME. Price the whole package on both sides before concluding which one "pays more."

---

## The structural difference

A **W-2 employee** trades some headline pay for infrastructure: the employer withholds taxes, pays the employer half of payroll taxes, and typically bundles health insurance, a retirement match, malpractice coverage, a CME allowance, and paid time off into the package.

A **1099 independent contractor** is a business of one. You invoice at a higher rate, and everything else is yours to arrange: quarterly estimated taxes, self-employment tax (both halves of payroll tax), occurrence-based malpractice, benefits, retirement, and any LLC or PLLC structure you practice under.

There is also a middle lane worth knowing: **contract W-2** roles. These are defined-term engagements booked through staffing agencies, with agency-provided malpractice and payroll-tax handling, and often short-term health coverage. You get contract-style rates with employee-style tax mechanics.

## The math that closes the gap

Work through a 1099 offer in this order before comparing it to a W-2 base:

1. **Self-employment tax.** You pay the employer half of payroll taxes yourself, money that a W-2 employer pays invisibly on top of your salary.
2. **Malpractice.** Occurrence-based coverage is your line item; W-2 and agency roles usually include it.
3. **Health insurance and retirement.** Self-funded premiums plus the employer retirement match you are not receiving.
4. **Paid time off.** A W-2 package with paid time off pays you for weeks that a contractor simply does not bill. Unbilled vacation, holidays, and sick days all come out of the hourly rate.
5. **CME, licensure, and admin time.** Courses, license renewals, credentialing paperwork, and bookkeeping are reimbursed or paid time in many W-2 roles and overhead in 1099 work.

Only after netting those out is the comparison accurate. A detailed walkthrough with worked examples is available in our [1099 vs W-2 guide](/resources/1099-vs-w2).

## When each structure wins

**1099 favors** clinicians who want control: multiple engagements, chosen schedules, deduction opportunities through business expenses, and rate negotiation per engagement. It rewards those who are comfortable running the business side, including invoicing, estimated taxes, and coverage gaps between contracts.

**W-2 favors** predictability: steady income, employer-subsidized benefits, malpractice handled, and no quarterly-tax administration. For most NPs building tenure, a strong W-2 package with real benefits outperforms a higher hourly rate once the contractor's cost stack comes out of it.

**Structure interacts with state rules, too.** Independent arrangements are simplest in full-practice-authority states (27 states + DC per AANP, 2025). In reduced- and restricted-practice states, a 1099 NP may also be arranging, and paying for, collaboration or supervision. Check your state in our [licensure guides](/resources) before assuming the contractor path is straightforward.

## Questions to ask before signing

- Is the quoted rate 1099 or W-2? If 1099, who carries malpractice, and is it occurrence-based or claims-made with a tail requirement?
- What is the guaranteed volume? A high hourly rate on unpredictable hours can net less than a lower guaranteed salary.
- Who pays for credentialing time, licensure, and CME?
- For contract W-2 roles, does the agency provide health coverage between assignments?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much more should a 1099 NP rate be than a W-2 salary?

There is no universal multiplier, but the 1099 rate must cover both halves of payroll tax, malpractice, health insurance, retirement, unpaid time off, and admin overhead before it matches a W-2 package. Net those costs out of the quoted hourly rate before comparing it to a W-2 offer.

### Do 1099 NPs need their own malpractice insurance?

Yes, almost always, and it is typically self-funded, occurrence-based coverage. W-2 employers and staffing agencies (for contract W-2 roles) usually provide malpractice as part of the package.

### What taxes do 1099 nurse practitioners pay?

Quarterly estimated federal (and applicable state) income taxes plus self-employment tax, which covers both the employee and employer halves of payroll taxes. Many contractors form an LLC or PLLC and work with an accountant on structure.

### What is the difference between contract W-2 and 1099?

Contract W-2 roles are defined-term agency engagements where the agency runs payroll taxes and provides malpractice. You get contractor-style rates with employee-style mechanics. Under a 1099 arrangement, you handle all of it yourself.

### Can new grad NPs take 1099 roles?

It is legal but rarely advisable. New grads benefit most from structured onboarding, supervision, and employer-carried malpractice, all of which are W-2 features. Build your clinical footing first; the contractor market will still be there.
