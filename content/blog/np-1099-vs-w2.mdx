**Quick answer:** 1099 NP work typically posts at $60–$150+ per hour versus full-time W-2 packages in the $110K–$170K range — but the hourly headline overstates the gap. As a 1099 contractor you self-fund both halves of payroll tax, your own malpractice coverage, health insurance, retirement, PTO, and CME. Price the whole package on both sides before concluding which "pays more."

## The structural difference

A **W-2 employee** trades some headline pay for infrastructure: the employer withholds taxes, pays the employer half of payroll taxes, and typically bundles health insurance, 401(k) match, malpractice coverage, CME allowance, and 3–4 weeks of PTO into the package.

A **1099 independent contractor** is a business of one. You invoice at a higher rate — $60–$150+ per hour is the typical band on this board — and everything else is yours to arrange: quarterly estimated taxes, self-employment tax (both halves of payroll tax), occurrence-based malpractice, benefits, retirement, and any LLC or PLLC structure you practice under.

There's also a middle lane worth knowing: **contract W-2** roles — defined-term engagements booked through staffing agencies, typically paying $70–$130 per hour with agency-provided malpractice and payroll-tax handling, and often short-term health coverage. You get contract-style rates with employee-style tax mechanics.

## The math that closes the gap

Work through a 1099 offer in this order before comparing it to a W-2 base:

1. **Self-employment tax.** You pay the employer half of payroll taxes yourself — money a W-2 employer pays invisibly on top of your salary.
2. **Malpractice.** Occurrence-based coverage is your line item; W-2 and agency roles usually include it.
3. **Health insurance and retirement.** Self-funded premiums plus the employer 401(k) match you're not receiving.
4. **Paid time off.** A W-2 package with 3–4 weeks of PTO pays you for weeks a contractor simply doesn't bill. Unbilled vacation, holidays, and sick days all come out of the hourly rate.
5. **CME, licensure, and admin time.** Courses, license renewals, credentialing paperwork, and bookkeeping — reimbursed or paid time in many W-2 roles, overhead in 1099 life.

Only after netting those out is the comparison honest. A detailed walkthrough with worked examples lives in our [1099 vs W-2 guide](/resources/1099-vs-w2).

## When each structure wins

**1099 favors** clinicians who want control — multiple engagements, chosen schedules, deduction opportunities through business expenses, and rate negotiation per engagement. It rewards those comfortable running the business side: invoicing, estimated taxes, and coverage gaps between contracts.

**W-2 favors** predictability — steady income, employer-subsidized benefits, malpractice handled, and zero quarterly-tax administration. For most NPs building tenure, a strong W-2 package in the $110K–$170K band with real benefits beats a mid-band hourly rate.

**Structure interacts with state rules, too.** Independent arrangements are simplest in full-practice-authority states (27 states + DC per AANP, 2025); in reduced- and restricted-practice states, a 1099 NP may also be arranging — and paying for — collaboration or supervision. Check your state in our [licensure guides](/resources) before assuming the contractor path is turnkey.

## Questions to ask before signing

- Is the rate quoted 1099 or W-2 — and if 1099, who carries malpractice, and is it occurrence-based or claims-made with a tail requirement?
- What's the guaranteed volume? A high hourly rate on unpredictable hours can net less than a lower guaranteed salary.
- Who pays for credentialing time, licensure, and CME?
- For contract W-2: does the agency provide health coverage between assignments?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much more should a 1099 NP rate be than a W-2 salary?

There's no universal multiplier, but the 1099 rate must cover both halves of payroll tax, malpractice, health insurance, retirement, unpaid time off, and admin overhead before it matches a W-2 package. Net those costs out of the $60–$150+ hourly band before comparing it to a $110K–$170K W-2 offer.

### Do 1099 NPs need their own malpractice insurance?

Yes, almost always — typically occurrence-based coverage, self-funded. W-2 employers and staffing agencies (for contract W-2 roles) usually provide malpractice as part of the package.

### What taxes do 1099 nurse practitioners pay?

Quarterly estimated federal (and applicable state) income taxes plus self-employment tax — both the employee and employer halves of payroll taxes. Many contractors form an LLC or PLLC and work with an accountant on structure.

### What's the difference between contract W-2 and 1099?

Contract W-2 roles are defined-term agency engagements, typically $70–$130 per hour, where the agency runs payroll taxes and provides malpractice — contractor-style rates with employee-style mechanics. 1099 means you handle all of it yourself.

### Can new grad NPs take 1099 roles?

It's legal but rarely advisable: new grads benefit most from structured onboarding, supervision, and employer-carried malpractice — all W-2 features. Build clinical footing first; the contractor market will still be there.
