**Quick answer:** As of October 2026, the nurse practitioner job outlook is strong nationally but uneven locally. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecopro.nr0.htm) projects NP employment to grow 41% from 2025 to 2035, the fastest of any detailed occupation. However, [HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/nursing-projections-factsheet.pdf) projects national NP supply above demand through 2038 and calls distribution the most important issue, so prospects depend heavily on location.

## What does BLS project for nurse practitioner jobs?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects that nurse practitioner employment will grow 41.0% from 2025 to 2035, from 336,300 jobs to 474,100, the [fastest growth of any detailed occupation](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/fastest-growing-occupations.htm). The figure comes from the [Employment Projections release of August 27, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecopro.nr0.htm) (USDL-26-1422). Over the same decade, BLS projects total US employment to grow 3.5%.

A nurse practitioner (NP) is an advanced practice registered nurse (APRN): a registered nurse with graduate preparation who assesses, diagnoses and manages patients within the scope their state allows. In the [Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm) (updated August 27, 2026), BLS groups NPs with nurse anesthetists and nurse midwives and projects about 32,200 openings a year for the three roles combined, on average, over the decade.

BLS names the drivers. Growth is expected "to stem from both the aging population and the increasing prevalence of chronic health conditions, such as heart disease, cancer, and diabetes," according to the release. The handbook adds that APRNs "will increasingly be used in team-based models of care, particularly in hospitals, offices of physicians, clinics, and other ambulatory care settings."

## What a projection can and cannot tell you

A BLS projection is a national, ten-year estimate of how many jobs an occupation will have. It is not a forecast of hiring in your city, in your specialty, or in this graduation season. BLS says so itself: the precision of its figures "does not account for the inherent uncertainty of predicting long-term changes in the labor market."

Four limits matter when you read the BLS projection of 41% growth from 2025 to 2035:

1. **It counts jobs, not job seekers.** An increase of 137,800 NP jobs says nothing about how many new NPs will compete for them.
2. **It is national.** A fast-growing occupation can still have more applicants than openings in a particular metro.
3. **It covers the NP occupation as a whole.** BLS publishes no separate projection for family, psychiatric, acute care or any other population focus.
4. **Different sources count different things.** BLS projections count jobs, the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) counts full-time equivalents of 40 hours a week, and the American Association of Nurse Practitioners (AANP) counts licensed NPs. These numbers cannot be subtracted from one another to measure a surplus.

## Are nurse practitioners oversaturated?

No official measure of NP saturation exists, but the main federal supply model projects more NPs than projected demand at the national level, and the education pipeline is still growing. Those are the strongest arguments behind the saturation worry, and they deserve a fair hearing.

**HRSA's supply and demand model.** The National Center for Health Workforce Analysis at HRSA published [Nurse Workforce Projections, 2023 to 2038](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/nursing-projections-factsheet.pdf) in December 2025. It states: "At the national level, the supply of nurse practitioners (NPs) is projected to exceed demand over the projection period; however, distribution remains the most important issue." For 2038 it projects an NP supply of 766,260 full-time equivalents against demand of 437,330, a supply adequacy of 175%. The 2028 figure is 126%.

Read those figures as model outputs, not head counts of unemployed NPs. HRSA notes that full-time equivalents "may differ from estimates of the headcounts of the health workforce," and it publishes alternative scenarios, including one for improved access to care, on its [workforce projections dashboard](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/workforce-projections) (data as of December 18, 2025).

**A growing pipeline.** The American Association of Colleges of Nursing (AACN) [reported on May 7, 2026](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/all-news/article/schools-of-nursing-see-enrollment-increases-across-most-program-levels-signaling-strong-interest-in-nursing-careers-1480) that enrollment in nursing master's programs rose 6.8% and enrollment in Doctor of Nursing Practice (DNP) programs rose 5.9% in its latest survey. Those totals include nursing graduate students outside NP tracks, so they indicate direction rather than an exact count of future NPs.

**A large licensed workforce.** AANP put the number of licensed NPs at more than 461,000 in November 2025, as [quoted by the UCLA School of Nursing](https://nursing.ucla.edu/index.php/news/ucla-nursing-celebrates-nurse-practitioners-during). That counts licenses held, not jobs filled.

**A cooling health care labor market.** In the [Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_09292026.htm) released September 29, 2026, seasonally adjusted openings in health care and social assistance fell to 1,359,000 in August from 1,474,000 in July, close to the 1,366,000 of August 2025. Layoffs and discharges in the sector rose to 199,000 from 149,000 in July. Both August figures are preliminary.

In the [September jobs report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm) released October 2, 2026, health care added 17,000 jobs, "at a slower pace than the average monthly gain over the prior 12 months (+33,000)." Both series cover whole sectors, including nonclinical jobs, and one month is not a trend.

## Are nurse practitioners still in demand?

Yes, on several measures: BLS projects the fastest job growth of any occupation, health care employment is still rising, and NPs top at least one major recruiter's list of requested searches.

- **Fastest projected growth.** The BLS projection above ranks NPs first among all detailed occupations for 2025 to 2035.
- **Recruiting requests.** AMN Healthcare's [2026 review of recruiting incentives](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/amn-insights/physician/whitepapers/2026-review-of-physician-and-advanced-practitioner-recruiting-incentives/) (September 16, 2026) reports that "for the sixth consecutive year, nurse practitioners (NPs) topped the list of most requested search engagements." That reflects 1,293 permanent physician and advanced practitioner searches conducted by one recruiting firm, not the whole market.
- **Limits on supply growth.** The same AACN release reports that 93,176 qualified applications were not accepted across nursing programs, including 6,496 to master's programs and 9,859 to DNP programs. AACN cites "insufficient clinical placement sites, faculty, preceptors, and classroom space, as well as budget cuts."
- **Outpatient growth.** In September 2026, ambulatory health care services added 13,000 jobs and hospitals 12,000, while nursing and residential care facilities lost 9,000, according to the jobs report. BLS expects APRNs to be used more in ambulatory and team-based settings.

## Signals for and against saturation, side by side

Both sets of signals hold at once, because they measure different things at different scales.

## Where is demand for nurse practitioners strongest?

The need is greatest where patients lack clinicians, which is why HRSA calls distribution, not the national total, the most important issue for NPs.

**Shortage areas.** HRSA's [Designated HPSA Quarterly Summary](https://data.hrsa.gov/Default/GenerateHPSAQuarterlyReport) counted 8,830 primary care Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) designations covering 102,257,076 people as of September 30, 2026. An HPSA is a geographic area, population group or facility that HRSA designates as short of primary care, dental or mental health clinicians. One caution: HRSA states that the primary care formula "does not take into account the availability of additional primary care services provided by nurse practitioners and physician assistants," so an HPSA is a signal of need, not a count of NP vacancies. The same report shows mental health HPSAs covering 154,557,656 people, though most of those designations rest on the ratio of population to psychiatrists. You can look up designated areas through HRSA's [shortage area tools](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/shortage-areas).

**Settings.** BLS points to hospitals, physician offices, clinics and other ambulatory settings, and ambulatory care led health care job gains in September 2026. Health Center Program grantees and Indian Health Service facilities are among the sites designated as HPSAs automatically under statute, according to the same HRSA report. Browse [community health NP jobs](/jobs/community-health) alongside [primary care roles](/jobs/primary-care).

**Practice authority.** Full practice authority lets NPs practice without a required physician agreement. It is granted in 27 states + DC (AANP, 2026), according to the [AANP state practice environment map](https://www.aanp.org/advocacy/state/state-practice-environment), though many of those states still require transition-to-practice hours before an NP practices independently. The handbook notes that as states change these laws, "APRNs are being allowed to perform more services." Check your state on the [scope of practice reference](/scope-of-practice), and read what full practice authority means in the [FPA guide](/resources/fpa-guide).

**Specialties.** No public source measures NP supply against openings by specialty, so we do not label any specialty saturated or undersupplied. For a dated view of which specialties and states employers are posting for on NP Hiring, read the [State of NP Hiring 2026 report](/reports/state-of-np-hiring-2026), and compare live listings in hubs such as [psychiatric mental health](/jobs/psychiatric-mental-health).

## What new grads and students can do now

National growth will not place you in a job; a deliberate search will. These steps respond directly to the evidence above.

1. **Widen your map.** HRSA's own conclusion is that distribution, not total supply, is the issue. If your metro is crowded, compare openings in shortage areas and nearby states. Practicing in another state requires that state's NP license, so check the requirements with the [licensure checker](/tools/licensure-checker) first.
2. **Consider a fellowship or residency.** Some employers run structured postgraduate programs for new NPs; see how they work and how to compare them in our [guide to NP fellowship and residency programs](/blog/np-fellowship-residency-programs).
3. **Use your RN experience as evidence.** If you have RN experience with the population you plan to treat, lead with it in your CV, cover letter and interviews.
4. **Start before graduation, and let listings find you.** Browse [new grad NP jobs](/jobs/new-grad) and set up [job alerts](/job-alerts) by specialty or keyword and location, so matching postings reach your inbox on the schedule you choose.
5. **Know the pay range before an offer.** Use the [salary guide](/salary-guide) and the pay stated in live postings, then read our walkthrough of [landing a first NP job as a new grad](/blog/new-grad-np-first-job).

Program directors can share the same sources with students: national growth and local competition are both real.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are nurse practitioners in demand in 2026?

By the main federal long-term measure, yes. BLS projects NP employment to grow 41% from 2025 to 2035, the fastest of any detailed occupation, in projections released August 27, 2026. Demand is uneven, though. HRSA projects national NP supply above demand through 2038 and calls distribution the most important issue, so prospects vary by location, setting and specialty.

### Is the nurse practitioner job market saturated?

There is no official saturation measure. HRSA projects national NP supply to exceed demand through 2038, and nursing graduate enrollment is rising, which supports concern about competition in some markets. BLS still projects the fastest job growth of any detailed occupation, and more than 102 million people lived in primary care shortage areas as of September 30, 2026. The evidence points to a market that is crowded in some places and short of clinicians in others.

### What is the job outlook for nurse practitioners?

BLS projects NP employment to rise 41.0% from 2025 to 2035, from 336,300 to 474,100 jobs, the fastest growth of all detailed occupations. BLS links the growth to an aging population and more chronic disease. A projection is a national estimate over ten years, not a forecast of hiring in any city or specialty.

### Which NP specialties are most in demand?

No public source measures NP supply against openings by specialty, and BLS projects the NP occupation as a whole. Federal shortage data points to primary care and mental health: HRSA counts large populations in both kinds of shortage area, though those designations are based mainly on physician ratios. For current employer demand, compare specialty hubs and the board's dated hiring report.

### Is it hard for new grad NPs to find a job?

No public source measures how long new graduate NPs take to find work, so the honest answer is that it depends on location and specialty. HRSA projects national NP supply above demand and calls distribution the main issue, while shortage areas still lack clinicians. New graduates can widen their search area, apply to fellowship or residency programs, use RN experience in the same population, and start before graduation.

### How many nurse practitioners are there in the US?

AANP reported more than 461,000 licensed NPs in November 2025, as quoted by the UCLA School of Nursing. BLS, which counts jobs rather than licenses, estimated 336,300 NP jobs in 2025 in its Employment Projections. The two figures measure different things, and not every licensed NP holds an NP job at a given time.
