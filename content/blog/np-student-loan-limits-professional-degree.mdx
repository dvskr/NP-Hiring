**Quick answer:** As of October 17, 2026, a nurse practitioner program counts as a professional degree for federal loan limits if it is a nursing MSN or DNP program; nurse anesthesia (DNAP) programs count too. That status rests on a June 24, 2026 court stay of part of an Education Department rule. While it lasts, eligible students can borrow up to $50,000 a year and $200,000 in total. The case is undecided.

---

## What changed for graduate loans on July 1, 2026?

Grad PLUS ended for new borrowers, and federal borrowing for graduate school became capped under a two-tier system. Public Law 119-21, signed on July 4, 2025, made the change in [Section 81001](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm). The Education Department now calls the law the Working Families Tax Cuts Act and says it [previously referred to it](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/01/2026-08556/reimagining-and-improving-student-education-federal-student-loan-program-final-regulations) as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

Grad PLUS was the federal loan that let graduate students borrow up to the full cost of attendance. Under the amended statute, [20 U.S.C. 1087e](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1087e), a graduate or professional student cannot receive one "for any period of instruction beginning on or after July 1, 2026," subject to the transition rule below. What remains is the Direct Unsubsidized Loan, with separate "graduate" and "professional" limits, reduced in proportion for less than full-time enrollment.

The statute defines a professional student by pointing to the professional degree definition in 34 CFR 668.2 "as in effect on July 4, 2025." That cross reference is the center of the litigation.

| Student type (loans for periods starting July 1, 2026 or later) | Annual Direct Unsubsidized limit | Aggregate limit | Grad PLUS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graduate student, new borrower | $20,500 | $100,000 (in addition to undergraduate loans) | Not available |
| Professional student, new borrower | $50,000 | $200,000 (in addition to undergraduate loans) | Not available |
| Graduate or professional student under the interim exception (enrolled and borrowing before July 1, 2026) | $20,500 | $138,500 (includes undergraduate loans) | Up to cost of attendance minus other aid, for a limited time |
| Every student borrower (lifetime) | Not applicable | $257,500 (repaid or forgiven loans still count) | Not applicable |

Figures are from the statute and the Department's [loan limits FAQ](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf) dated May 20, 2026. Parent PLUS loans taken for a dependent student do not count toward the lifetime maximum, which does not apply during the interim exception.

## What did the Education Department's rule do to nursing degrees?

The final rule left graduate nursing in the lower "graduate" tier. The Department published it on May 1, 2026 ([91 FR 23768](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/01/2026-08556/reimagining-and-improving-student-education-federal-student-loan-program-final-regulations)), effective July 1, 2026.

As reproduced in the court's [June 24, 2026 opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_26-cv-01780/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_26-cv-01780-0.pdf), the new definition had two parts. Part (i) set requirements, among them that the degree is "generally at the doctoral level," takes at least six academic years of postsecondary coursework, and carries a four-digit Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) code in the same group as the listed fields. Part (ii) listed eleven fields: pharmacy, dentistry, veterinary medicine, chiropractic, law, medicine, optometry, osteopathic medicine, podiatry, theology and clinical psychology. The preamble added that the degree must not lead to work that ordinarily must be supervised by a licensed professional in a different occupation.

Nursing was not on the list, and, as the court summarized the rule, "All other degrees will be considered 'graduate' degrees." For nursing students starting after July 1, 2026, that meant the $20,500 annual limit.

## What did the June 24, 2026 court stay change?

The stay suspended the rule's new definition, and the Department now treats MSN, DNP and DNAP programs as awarding professional degrees while the stay lasts. In *American Association of Nurse Practitioners v. McMahon* (D.D.C. No. 26-1780, consolidated with a physician assistant case, No. 26-1941), Judge Beryl A. Howell found the plaintiffs "likely to succeed on their APA claim that the Rule's definition of 'professional degree' is contrary to law," reasoning that Congress intended the Department to apply the preexisting definition.

The order was narrow. Under 5 U.S.C. 705, the court stayed part (i) and the free from supervision requirement "pending final resolution of this litigation," but not the list of eleven fields. It denied all other relief, including requests that would in effect have reinstated uncapped borrowing: the litigation "cannot remedy plaintiffs' primary frustration over the elimination of uncapped borrowing." The stay did not bring back Grad PLUS.

The Department responded with electronic announcement [GENERAL-26-42](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-06-29/update-list-professional-degree-programs-due-court-order-updated-july-10-2026), dated June 29, 2026 and updated July 10, 2026. It says the Department is confident its rule is lawful and will keep defending it, but will treat the listed programs as professional "for the duration of the Court's stay." Three nursing entries are listed:

- 51.3801, Registered Nursing/Registered Nurse (MSN)
- 51.3804, Nurse Anesthetist (DNAP)
- 51.3818, Nursing Practice (DNP)

The July 10 update says the MSN and DNP codes "include any program within the same four-digit CIP code," as assigned by the school, "provided it awards the same credential." So an NP program coded under 51.38 that awards an MSN or a DNP appears to fall inside the list; the school assigns the code. The designations "may change as litigation in the case proceeds." As of October 17, 2026, this is still the latest professional degree announcement on the Department's [list of guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/topics/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-information/dear-colleague-letters-and-electronic-announcements) for the 2025 law.

## Which cases are still pending, and what are the next dates?

At least five federal cases challenging the definition are open, and none has reached a final judgment. The table covers three earlier cases, and of those only AANP v. McMahon has changed how loans are administered. Two later cases are also pending. Naturopathic medicine groups sued in the District of Oregon on July 31, 2026 ([Association of Accredited Naturopathic Medical Colleges v. McMahon](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73706367/association-of-accredited-naturopathic-medical-colleges-v-mcmahon/), No. 3:26-cv-01599), and that court heard a preliminary injunction motion on September 23, 2026. On October 16, 2026 it granted a preliminary injunction that requires the Department to treat the naturopathic doctor (N.D.) degree as a professional degree for the plaintiff groups and their members, and it denied the government's request for a stay pending appeal. That order covers naturopathic students only; it does not change how nurse practitioner programs are treated. Labor unions, including National Nurses United, sued in the District of Columbia on August 11, 2026 ([AFL-CIO v. Department of Education](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74219096/american-federation-of-labor-and-congress-of-industrial-organizations-v/), No. 1:26-cv-02833), and that case is also assigned to Judge Howell.

## What happens to students who were enrolled before July 1, 2026?

They may keep the old rules, including Grad PLUS, for a limited period. The Department calls this the interim exception, and its [May 20, 2026 FAQ](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf) sets out the conditions:

1. The student was enrolled in a program of study at a school as of June 30, 2026.
2. The student received (was disbursed) a Direct Loan for that same program before July 1, 2026. Any Direct Loan counts; it did not need to be Grad PLUS.
3. The old limits last for the "expected time to credential": the lesser of three academic years or the program's length minus the time completed before July 1, 2026.

For graduate students, a program of study is defined by the school, the four-digit CIP code and the credential level, so moving from an MSN into a separate DNP program generally means a new program under the new limits. Withdrawing from all coursework ends the exception; dropping one course does not. A qualifying student cannot opt out to take the $50,000 professional limit: a borrower "is not permitted to opt out of the interim exception." Grad PLUS borrowed under the exception counts toward the $257,500 lifetime maximum once the exception ends.

## What NP, CRNA and CNM students and programs can do now

Start with the financial aid office, because the classification depends on your exact program code and credential. These are questions to ask, not borrowing advice.

1. **Which CIP code and credential level** does your program report, is it on the Department's interim professional list, and which annual limit applies for 2026 to 2027?
2. **Do you qualify for the interim exception**, and how much of your expected time to credential remains?
3. **Has the school set a lower program limit?** Since July 1, 2026, schools may set lower annual limits for a program if they apply them consistently to every student in it ([Dear Colleague Letter GEN-26-02](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2026-06-26/implementing-new-institutional-authority-set-program-level-federal-student-loan-limits), June 26, 2026).
4. **What happens if the classification changes** during an academic year, as the Department says it may?
5. **Check your own loan record** at [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/), and treat Department announcements, not social posts, as the reference point.

If you are still choosing a program, weigh financing next to accreditation and clinical placement support ([how to evaluate NP programs](/blog/how-to-evaluate-np-programs); [how to become a nurse practitioner](/blog/how-to-become-a-nurse-practitioner)). Graduating students can browse [new graduate NP jobs](/jobs/new-grad) and [fellowship and residency programs](/blog/np-fellowship-residency-programs), set [job alerts](/job-alerts), and check each posting for tuition or loan repayment terms. ## Can an employer help repay NP student loans?

Yes. Federal tax law lets an employer pay part of an employee's student loans through an educational assistance program under Internal Revenue Code Section 127. According to the IRS's April 2026 fact sheet [FS-2026-10](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/updates-to-frequently-asked-questions-about-educational-assistance-programs), the amount an employee can exclude from income for "payments of principal or interest on qualified education loans and other educational assistance combined is $5,250 per calendar year (adjusted for increases in the cost of living for taxable years beginning after 2026)."

The IRS lists conditions: a separate written plan for the exclusive benefit of employees, no discrimination in favor of officers, shareholders or highly compensated employees, and a cap on benefits going to owners of more than 5 percent of the business. Plan design and tax treatment are questions for a benefits or tax adviser.

For recruiting, put the benefit in the posting: the amount, any service commitment, and whether it covers tuition, loans or both. Candidates can only compare what is written down. Employers can [post a nurse practitioner job](/post-job) with these terms and find hiring guides in [employer resources](/for-employers/resources).

## What could the loan limits mean for the NP pipeline?

National enrollment data do not yet cover the period after the new limits took effect. The American Association of Colleges of Nursing (AACN) released its annual figures on [May 7, 2026](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/all-news/article/schools-of-nursing-see-enrollment-increases-across-most-program-levels-signaling-strong-interest-in-nursing-careers-1480), from a survey conducted in fall 2025. Master's enrollment rose 6.8% to 146,718 students and DNP enrollment rose 5.9% to 44,976. Schools also turned away 93,176 qualified applications, citing "insufficient clinical placement sites, faculty, preceptors, and classroom space, as well as budget cuts."

Deans expected the caps to hurt. In AACN's [November 2025 survey](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/0/PDFs/Data/AACN-Data-Loan-Cap-Survey-Report-December-2025.pdf), answered by 469 of 909 member schools invited, 78% said the $20,500 annual cap would negatively affect their post-baccalaureate enrollment. That was a forecast by deans, made before the court stay, not a measured result. For the demand side of the market, see the [nurse practitioner job outlook](/blog/nurse-practitioner-job-outlook).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is nurse practitioner a professional degree for federal student loans?

As of October 17, 2026, yes for loan limits, but only provisionally. A June 24, 2026 court stay in AANP v. McMahon suspended the Education Department's narrower definition, and the Department's announcement GENERAL-26-42 (updated July 10, 2026) treats nursing programs awarding an MSN or DNP, and DNAP programs, as professional for the duration of the stay. The Department says this may change as the litigation proceeds.

### How much can NP students borrow in federal loans in 2026?

It depends on classification and timing. A new borrower in a program treated as professional can receive up to $50,000 a year in Direct Unsubsidized Loans, with a $200,000 aggregate limit. A graduate tier student is limited to $20,500 a year and $100,000. Every student faces a $257,500 lifetime maximum. Students enrolled and borrowing before July 1, 2026 may keep the old rules for a limited time.

### Did Grad PLUS loans end for nursing students?

Yes, for new borrowers. Under 20 U.S.C. 1087e, a graduate or professional student cannot receive a Grad PLUS loan for any period of instruction beginning on or after July 1, 2026. Students enrolled in a program on June 30, 2026 who had received a Direct Loan for it before July 1 can keep borrowing Grad PLUS for that program for up to three academic years.

### Does the court stay mean nursing counts as a professional degree?

Only for now. The June 24, 2026 order is a stay pending final resolution, not a final judgment. The Department responded by treating MSN, DNP and DNAP programs as professional. Summary judgment briefing in that case is scheduled through December 4, 2026, at least four other cases are pending, and the stay did not restore Grad PLUS.

### What happens to students who were already enrolled before July 1, 2026?

A student enrolled in a program on June 30, 2026 who received any Direct Loan for it before July 1 qualifies for the interim exception: the pre-July 2026 limits, including Grad PLUS, for the lesser of three academic years or the remaining program length. The student cannot opt out to take the $50,000 professional limit, and changing programs or withdrawing ends the exception.

### Is a CRNA (DNAP or DNP) program a professional degree?

During the stay, yes. The Department's interim list includes Nurse Anesthetist (DNAP) under CIP 51.3804 and Nursing Practice (DNP) under CIP 51.3818, which covers programs in the same four-digit CIP code that award a DNP. The American Association of Nurse Anesthesiology is a plaintiff in a separate Massachusetts case. A financial aid office can confirm how a program is coded.

### Can an employer help repay NP student loans?

Yes. Under Internal Revenue Code Section 127, an employer's educational assistance program can pay principal or interest on an employee's qualified education loans. The IRS says up to $5,250 per calendar year of combined assistance can be excluded from income, adjusted for cost of living for tax years beginning after 2026. The plan must be written and must not favor highly compensated employees.
