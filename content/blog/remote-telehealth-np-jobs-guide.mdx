## Remote vs telehealth: the distinction that matters

Job boards use the labels loosely, but there is a real difference. **Telehealth** roles are patient-facing by video and phone: scheduled visits, asynchronous messaging, e-prescribing, and documentation between sessions. **Remote** is the broader umbrella. It includes telehealth but also utilization review, care management, and other non-visit clinical work done from home. When you filter [remote NP jobs](/jobs/remote) or [telehealth NP jobs](/jobs/telehealth), read the day-to-day description, not just the tag.

## Licensure: the rule that shapes everything

The rule that governs every virtual role: you must be licensed in the state where the **patient** is located at the time of the visit. A telehealth panel covering patients in four states means credentials for four states.

This is where the **Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC)** earns its keep. The compact covers the RN license that underpins your APRN credential: a multistate RN license issued by your compact home state is recognized across member states. Your APRN license, though, is still issued state by state, so telehealth employers hiring across many states either sponsor additional state licenses or prioritize clinicians who already hold them. Several large states, including California and New York, are not compact members and always require their own licenses.

Two practical implications:

- If a role covers non-compact states, budget real lead time for endorsement applications before your start date.
- Each additional state license you hold makes you more valuable to telehealth employers, because you widen the panel they can assign you. Our [state licensure guides](/resources) cover the path for every state.

## What the home setup requires

Employers standardize on a HIPAA-compliant workspace: a private room where screens and conversations cannot be observed, a wired or reliable high-speed connection, and employer-provided or approved software for the EHR and video platform. Expect equipment stipends at larger platforms and bring-your-own hardware at smaller ones. Ask, because it changes your effective compensation.

## What remote and telehealth NP roles pay

Remote and telehealth roles are priced by the same factors as on-site roles: the state your patients are in, the setting, the specialty, and whether the role is W-2 or 1099. The U.S. median for nurse practitioners is $129,210 (BLS OEWS, May 2024). Telehealth-heavy platforms often structure pay per visit or hourly, and independent-contractor (1099) arrangements are common, with the usual contractor caveats on taxes, malpractice, and benefits (see our [1099 vs W-2 explainer](/blog/np-1099-vs-w2)). For the live median in a given state, where this board publishes one, use the [salary guide](/salary-guide), and filter [remote NP jobs](/jobs/remote) for the posted figures behind it.

Demand is durable: BLS projects about 40% employment growth for nurse practitioners from 2024 to 2034, and virtual care lets employers hire wherever the licenses are.

## How to position yourself

1. **Anchor on your home-state license first.** If you live in a compact state, secure the multistate RN license; then build APRN licenses in your target coverage states.
2. **Document your telehealth competencies.** Video-visit experience, asynchronous triage, e-prescribing workflows, and EHR fluency belong on the resume explicitly.
3. **Mind practice authority.** Full-practice-authority states (27 states + DC per AANP, 2026) simplify a telehealth employer's supervision logistics, which is why FPA-state licenses are often listed as preferred.
4. **Screen the role structure.** Ask about panel size, visits per hour, documentation time, after-hours messaging expectations, and whether equipment and licensure costs are reimbursed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can nurse practitioners work fully remote?

Yes. Telehealth visit work, utilization review, and care management are all done from home. Patient-facing telehealth requires licensure in each state where your patients are located, plus a HIPAA-compliant home workspace.

### Do I need a license in every state I see patients in?

Yes. Licensure follows the patient's location, not yours. The Nurse Licensure Compact simplifies the RN layer across member states, but APRN licenses are issued individually by each state.

### How much do telehealth NPs make?

Telehealth pay is set by state, setting, specialty, and employment structure, not by the remote label. The national NP median is $129,210 (BLS OEWS, May 2024). 1099 telehealth work is quoted hourly before self-funded taxes, malpractice, and benefits, so net those out before comparing it to a W-2 salary.

### What is the outlook for telehealth NP work?

Nurse practitioner employment overall is projected to grow about 40% from 2024 to 2034 (BLS Employment Projections), and telehealth lets employers recruit across state lines, so multi-state-licensed NPs are in demand.

### What equipment do telehealth employers require?

A private, HIPAA-compliant workspace and reliable high-speed internet are universal. Larger platforms usually ship hardware or pay an equipment stipend; smaller practices may expect you to supply your own. Confirm before accepting.
