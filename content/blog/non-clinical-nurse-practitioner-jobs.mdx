**Quick answer:** Non-clinical nurse practitioner jobs use your clinical judgment without a patient panel: utilization review and chart review for health plans, case management, clinical documentation integrity, pharmaceutical and biotech roles, clinical research, informatics, teaching, quality, legal nurse consulting, and public health. Each employer decides whether a role is remote, and many credentials in these fields rest on the RN license rather than the NP license.

---

## What counts as a non-clinical NP job?

A non-clinical NP job is one where your clinical knowledge is the product but you are not the treating clinician: you review, document, teach, analyze, or advise. The line is not sharp. Some roles in this guide involve no patients at all, such as chart review. Others are better called indirect care: a clinical research investigator still sees study participants, and NP faculty who teach clinical courses are expected to keep practicing.

That spectrum shapes two decisions: whether you must keep your license and certification active, and whether the role can be remote. Work done in records and data can be done from home when the employer allows it; work with people usually needs you on site. Seeing patients from home is telehealth, a different market covered in the [remote and telehealth NP jobs guide](/blog/remote-telehealth-np-jobs-guide).

## Health plan and documentation roles

These roles turn clinical judgment into decisions about coverage, coding, and records, working from charts rather than with patients.

### Utilization review and management

Utilization review checks whether a requested or delivered service meets medical necessity criteria; utilization management is the wider program, including prior authorization. For Medicare Advantage, when a plan expects a partially or fully adverse medical necessity decision, [a physician or other appropriate health care professional](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.566) with expertise suited to the services at issue must review it (42 CFR 422.566(d)). Which reviews an NP may perform is set by the plan, so check the posting.

Clocks shape the job. Under the [CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-and-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f) (CMS-0057-F, issued January 17, 2024), beginning January 1, 2026, Medicare Advantage organizations and Medicaid and CHIP programs and managed care plans must send decisions on prior authorization requests for items and services other than drugs within 72 hours for expedited requests and seven calendar days for standard requests. Payers covered by the rule must also give a specific reason when they deny a request. The decision timeframes do not apply to plans sold on the federally facilitated exchanges.

### Chart review and risk adjustment

Chart review confirms that what was billed or coded is documented. In Medicare Advantage, risk adjustment ties plan payment partly to enrollees' diagnoses, and through its Risk Adjustment Data Validation program [CMS confirms that diagnoses submitted for risk adjustment are supported in the enrollees' medical records](https://www.cms.gov/data-research/monitoring-programs/medicare-risk-adjustment-data-validation-program). Years of documenting chronic disease are the core skill.

### Case management

Case managers assess members or patients, coordinate services, and manage transitions. For the board-certified case manager (CCM) credential, the Commission for Case Manager Certification's [eligibility summary](https://yourcommission.org/blog/what-you-need-know-about-eligibility-you-apply-ccmr-exam-fast-facts) (posted May 2019) asks for either a current, unrestricted license or certification in a health or human services discipline, with the RN among its examples, or a bachelor's or graduate degree in a qualifying field. Candidates also need full-time case management employment in the last five years: 12 months supervised by a CCM, 24 months without CCM supervision, or 12 months supervising case managers.

### Clinical documentation integrity

Clinical documentation integrity (CDI) specialists review inpatient records during or after the stay and query clinicians when documentation falls short. The Association of Clinical Documentation Integrity Specialists' Certified Clinical Documentation Specialist (CCDS) [eligibility](https://www.acdis.org/certification/ccds/requirements) lists an RN, RHIA, RHIT, MD or DO plus two years as a documentation specialist in an inpatient acute care facility, and candidates must currently work in CDI. The list does not name the NP credential, so an NP would apply through the RN route.

## Pharmaceutical, biotech and clinical research roles

Industry roles cover research, safety, and scientific communication; some stay close to patients and others never touch them.

**Clinical research coordination.** Coordinators run trials at a research site: screening, consent workflows, visits, and data. The Association of Clinical Research Professionals' Certified Clinical Research Coordinator (CCRC) [requires 3,000 hours of verifiable work experience](https://acrpnet.org/certification/crc-certification); ACRP may grant one 1,500 hour waiver to applicants who hold an active ACRP certification or have completed a clinical research education program.

**Clinical research investigator roles.** Investigators and subinvestigators conduct a study at their site, which is indirect care, not desk work. FDA rules say a sponsor [shall select only investigators qualified by training and experience](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/312.53) as appropriate experts to investigate the drug, and the investigator's signed Form FDA 1572 lists the subinvestigators assisting (21 CFR 312.53). What an NP may do on a protocol also depends on state scope of practice; check yours with the [licensure checker](/tools/licensure-checker).

**Drug safety and medical information.** Drug safety (pharmacovigilance) staff assess adverse event reports. For investigational drugs, the sponsor [must promptly review all information relevant to the safety of the drug](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/312.32) and file IND safety reports with FDA and investigators no later than 15 calendar days after deciding a report is required (21 CFR 312.32). Medical information teams answer clinicians' product questions. Requirements vary, so check the posting.

**Medical science liaison.** A medical science liaison (MSL) builds relationships with leading physicians, researchers, and clinicians and serves as a scientific resource, usually concentrating on one therapeutic area or disease state. [Explore Health Careers](https://explorehealthcareers.org/career/pharmacology/medical-science-liaison/) describes MSLs as typically holding a doctorate such as a PhD, PharmD, or MD, and notes that some enter with a nursing background that includes therapeutic area expertise. Read each posting's degree requirement before applying.

## Informatics, health technology and AI work

Clinical informatics roles shape the tools clinicians use: EHR build, order sets, decision support, and evaluation of new clinical software, at health systems and technology companies. The American Nurses Credentialing Center's [Informatics Nursing certification (NI-BC)](https://www.nursingworld.org/our-certifications/informatics-nurse/) requires a current, active RN license, a bachelor's or higher degree in nursing, two years of full-time RN practice, 30 hours of informatics continuing education in three years, and informatics practice hours or a graduate informatics program.

AI work deserves care. FDA keeps a [list of AI-enabled medical devices authorized for marketing](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/list-artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices) in the United States, so some clinical AI tools are regulated medical devices, and evaluating such tools is part of informatics work. If you see postings described as AI training or evaluation work, for example writing clinical questions or grading model answers, read them for what you will actually produce, whether it is contract work (see [1099 versus W-2](/resources/1099-vs-w2)), whether a license is required, and the confidentiality terms.

## Education, quality, legal and government roles

**Education.** NP faculty teach didactic and clinical courses and oversee students in placements. The National Task Force on Quality Nurse Practitioner Education's [Standards for Quality Nurse Practitioner Education](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/0/PDFs/CCNE/NTFS-NP-Final.pdf) (6th edition, February 2022) ask programs to document each NP faculty member's national certification and license or other authorization to practice as an NP, plus clinical practice for faculty teaching diagnosis and management or clinical courses. Clinical teaching means keeping a foot in practice.

**Quality and patient safety.** Quality roles measure care and run improvement projects. The National Association for Healthcare Quality's [Certified Professional in Healthcare Quality (CPHQ)](https://nahq.org/individuals/cphq-certification/) has no formal eligibility requirements, though NAHQ recommends at least two years in healthcare quality.

**Legal nurse consulting.** Legal nurse consultants review records and explain care for attorneys, insurers, and hospitals. To sit for the Legal Nurse Consultant Certified (LNCC) exam, the American Legal Nurse Consultant Certification Board [requires](https://aalnc.org/lncc-certification/) a full and unrestricted US RN license, at least five years of practice as an RN, and 2,000 hours of legal nurse consulting experience within the past five years. Those hours must be [performed at a client's request](https://aalnc.org/certification-faqs/) on claims or cases that need nursing education and experience.

**Public health and government.** Government nurse roles include public health programs and policy work. For federal positions in the Nurse Series 0610, the Office of Personnel Management's [qualification standard](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/classification-qualifications/general-schedule-qualification-standards/0600/nurse-series-0610/) requires a nursing degree or diploma from an accredited program, a passing score on the National Council Licensure Examination, and a current, active, full, and unrestricted license or registration as a professional nurse. Federal openings are posted on [USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov/about/), the federal government's official employment site.

## Do non-clinical NP jobs require an active license?

It depends on the role, and the posting is the authority. In the sources above, many credentials rest on the RN license, not the NP license: NI-BC, LNCC, CCDS, and federal nurse positions all name the RN, and CCM lists it among accepted licenses. NP faculty roles are the clear exception, because the NTF standards ask for NP certification and authorization to practice as an NP.

Two practical points follow. Because so many of these paths name the RN license, letting it lapse would close them. And before you let a national NP certification lapse, read your certifying body's renewal and reinstatement rules. The [NP certification overview](/blog/np-certification-overview) links each board.

## How to move from clinical practice into a non-clinical role

Choose one path, translate your experience into its vocabulary, and close one specific gap.

1. **Pick one target path.** Use the table above and the [specialty finder](/tools/specialty-finder) to match your strengths: documentation habits point to CDI or chart review, care coordination points to case management, protocol work points to research.
2. **Read ten real postings for that path.** List the requirements that repeat. That list, not a generic certification, is your gap analysis.
3. **Rewrite your resume for reviewers, not patients.** Lead with quality measures you owned, prior authorizations you handled, protocols or order sets you built, and students you precepted. The [NP resume guide](/blog/np-resume-guide) and [cover letter guide](/blog/np-cover-letter-guide) show how to state outcomes.
4. **Get adjacent experience inside your current job.** Join a documentation, quality, or EHR committee; volunteer as a preceptor or as a subinvestigator on a site study. Committee work will not meet eligibility hours on its own, but it gives you concrete examples for the resume and the interview.
5. **Pursue a credential only when its hours fit.** CCM, CCDS, CCRC, LNCC, and NI-BC all require hours, employment, or a supervised practicum in the field, so they follow a first role rather than open the door to it. CPHQ is the exception, with no formal eligibility requirement.
6. **Prepare for a different interview.** Be ready for case reviews and criteria questions; practice with the [NP interview questions](/blog/np-interview-questions) and adapt them to review work.

A hybrid path is also realistic: keep a clinical role while you build experience. NP Hiring is built around practicing NP roles and screens out teaching, management and recruiting titles, so use it mainly for the clinical half of that plan: browse [remote NP jobs](/jobs/remote) and [telehealth NP jobs](/jobs/telehealth) for patient-facing work with a flexible setup, and set up [job alerts](/job-alerts) so new listings reach you.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are non-clinical jobs for nurse practitioners?

Non-clinical NP jobs use clinical knowledge without direct patient care: utilization review, chart review and risk adjustment, case management, clinical documentation integrity, drug safety, medical science liaison, clinical research, informatics, teaching, quality improvement, legal nurse consulting, and public health. Some, such as research investigator and clinical faculty roles, still involve patients or clinical practice.

### Can a nurse practitioner work remotely without seeing patients?

Yes, in some roles. Work that lives in records and data, such as utilization review, chart review, and documentation review, can be done remotely when the employer allows it with secure system access. Each employer decides, and the posting states whether a role is remote, hybrid, or on site. Roles built on relationships or participants, such as research site work, usually involve being present.

### Do non-clinical NP jobs require an active NP license?

Not always, and the posting is the authority. Many credentials in this field are built on the RN license: ANCC's informatics certification requires an active RN license, the LNCC requires an unrestricted RN license, and federal nurse positions require a current RN license. NP faculty roles differ, because national standards ask programs to document NP certification and authorization to practice as an NP.

### How do I move from clinical practice into a non-clinical NP role?

Choose one path, read ten postings for it, and list the requirements that repeat. Rewrite your resume around quality, prior authorization, protocol, and teaching work rather than patient volume. Build adjacent experience on committees or as a preceptor while still employed, and add a credential once you have the field hours its eligibility rules require.

### Do pharmaceutical companies hire nurse practitioners?

Drug and biotech companies hire clinicians for clinical research, drug safety, medical information, and medical science liaison roles. Degree expectations vary: Explore Health Careers describes MSLs as typically holding a doctorate such as a PhD, PharmD, or MD, with some entering from nursing with therapeutic area expertise. Read each posting for its stated degree and license requirements.

### What does a utilization review nurse practitioner do?

A utilization review NP checks whether requested or delivered care meets medical necessity criteria, usually for a health plan. For Medicare Advantage, a physician or other appropriate health care professional must review any expected adverse medical necessity decision (42 CFR 422.566). Since January 1, 2026, a CMS rule has required Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, and CHIP plans to decide most non-drug prior authorization requests within 72 hours if expedited and seven calendar days if standard.
