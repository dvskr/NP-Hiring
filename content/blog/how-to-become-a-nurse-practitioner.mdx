**Quick answer:** To become a nurse practitioner, earn a BSN and RN license, finish an accredited MSN or DNP in one population focus, pass national certification and get a state APRN license. Counting a four-year BSN, a BSN-to-DNP path is about seven to eight years of school; a direct-entry master's takes about two to three years after another bachelor's.

---

## What are the requirements to become a nurse practitioner?

Nurse practitioner requirements come down to four gates: an RN license, an accredited graduate program, national certification and a state APRN license. The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) puts it plainly: "In general, APRNs must have a registered nursing license, complete an accredited graduate-level program, pass a national certification exam, and have an APRN license" ([BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm), last modified October 29, 2026). An APRN, or advanced practice registered nurse, is an RN with graduate preparation in an advanced role. Our guide to [what a nurse practitioner is](/resources/what-is-a-nurse-practitioner) covers the role itself, the [APRN guide](/blog/what-is-an-aprn) shows where NPs sit among the advanced practice roles, and the [NP vs PA comparison](/resources/np-vs-pa) helps if you are still choosing between the two careers.

In practice the path has eight steps:

| Step | What it involves | Who sets the rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Earn a BSN | A bachelor's degree in nursing, or another route into nursing | Each nursing school |
| 2. Get your RN license | Pass the NCLEX-RN after an approved nursing program | Your state board of nursing |
| 3. Build clinical experience | RN practice, if your NP program requires it | Each NP program |
| 4. Choose a population focus | FNP, AGPCNP, AGACNP, PNP, PMHNP, WHNP or NNP | National education and certification standards |
| 5. Finish an MSN or DNP | An accredited graduate NP program with supervised clinical hours | The program, its accreditor and the certifying board |
| 6. Pass national certification | The board exam for your population focus | AANPCB, ANCC, PNCB, NCC and other boards |
| 7. Get a state APRN license | The license or recognition to practice as an NP | Your state board of nursing |
| 8. Register to prescribe | DEA registration and any state prescribing registration | The DEA and your state |

## How long does it take to become a nurse practitioner?

It depends on where you start. We found no national body that publishes a single total, so the table below puts together the program lengths that BLS and the American Association of Colleges of Nursing (AACN) publish for each route. Certification, licensure and any RN experience your program requires come on top.

| Route | Who it fits | Education steps | Typical program length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional BSN to MSN | New nursing students and RNs with a BSN | BSN, NCLEX-RN, then an MSN NP program | BSN typically 4 years (BLS); the MSN length is set by each school |
| ADN or diploma to MSN | RNs without a bachelor's degree | An RN-to-MSN bridge, or RN-to-BSN and then an MSN | RN-to-MSN up to 2 to 3 years; RN-to-BSN 1 to 2 years (AACN) |
| Direct-entry MSN | People with a bachelor's degree in another field | An accelerated or entry-level master's, plus the NCLEX-RN | About 2 to 3 years (AACN) |
| BSN to DNP | RNs with a BSN who want the doctorate | BSN, NCLEX-RN, then a post-baccalaureate DNP | BSN typically 4 years (BLS); DNP three calendar years full-time, or four academic years (AACN, 2006) |

Put together, the doctoral route is about seven to eight years of school. BSN programs "typically take 4 years to complete" ([BLS: Registered Nurses](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm), last modified October 29, 2026), and AACN's DNP Essentials recommended that a post-baccalaureate DNP run "three calendar years, or 36 months of full-time study including summers or four years on a traditional academic calendar" ([AACN DNP Essentials](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/0/PDFs/Publications/DNPEssentials.pdf), October 2006). With a bachelor's degree in another field, AACN reports that fast-track master's programs generally take about 2 to 3 years ([AACN: Accelerated Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/accelerated-baccalaureate-and-masters-degrees-in-nursing), updated May 2026).

For a BSN followed by an MSN, neither BLS nor AACN publishes a typical length: each school sets its own plan of study, and part-time study takes longer. AACN's guide for graduate applicants tells you to ask how long the program is, what the credit load is and whether full-time and part-time options exist ([AACN: Your Guide to Graduate Nursing Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/42/Publications/Brochures/GradStudentsBrochure.pdf), 2024). Get the answer in writing, along with the expected completion date.

## Step 1: Earn a BSN, or take another route into nursing

Every NP route starts with an RN license, and BLS describes three education paths to it: "a bachelor's degree in nursing, an associate's degree in nursing, or a diploma from an approved nursing program" ([BLS: Registered Nurses](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm)).

If you already hold a bachelor's degree in another field, AACN reports that fast-track baccalaureate programs "take between 12 and 18 months to complete, including prerequisites" ([AACN: Accelerated Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/accelerated-baccalaureate-and-masters-degrees-in-nursing)). RNs with an associate degree or diploma can finish an RN-to-BSN program, which AACN says takes 1 to 2 years depending on the school ([AACN: Degree Completion Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/degree-completion-programs-for-rns), updated May 2026).

## Can you become a nurse practitioner without a BSN?

Yes, through two kinds of programs, but not without an RN license. Both FNP certifying boards require an active RN license, so every route still passes through the NCLEX-RN.

- **RN-to-MSN bridge.** AACN describes these programs as a way to "transition RNs with diplomas and associate degrees to the master's degree level" and says they generally take up to 2 to 3 years, depending on the school and your prior coursework. It counts 155 RN-to-MSN programs nationwide ([AACN: Degree Completion Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/degree-completion-programs-for-rns)).
- **Direct-entry MSN.** If your bachelor's degree is in another field, accelerated or entry-level master's programs, often called direct-entry programs, bring you into nursing at the master's level. AACN counted 104 of them in 2025 and says they generally take about 2 to 3 years ([AACN: Accelerated Programs](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/accelerated-baccalaureate-and-masters-degrees-in-nursing)).

AACN's fact sheet does not say that every accelerated master's program prepares nurse practitioners, so confirm that the track you choose is an NP population focus that makes you eligible for national certification. Our [program evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-np-programs) lists the questions to ask.

## Step 2: Pass the NCLEX-RN and get your RN license

After graduating, you take the NCLEX-RN, the licensure exam the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN) develops and its member boards use "to assist in making licensure decisions" ([NCLEX-RN Test Plan](https://www.nclex.com/files/2026_RN_Test%20Plan_English-F.pdf), effective April 2026). The license itself comes from your state board of nursing.

## Step 3: Build clinical experience as an RN

Programs differ here, and there is no single national rule. BLS lists a year of critical care experience as a requirement for nurse anesthetists, who "must have 1 year of experience working as registered nurse in a critical care setting," but it states no comparable rule for nurse practitioners ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm)). NP programs set their own admission rules, so whether you need RN experience first, and how much, depends on the program. Ask each school in writing before you apply.

## Step 4: Choose a population focus

Your NP program prepares you for one population focus, and that choice decides which certification exam you can take. The 2022 standards of the National Task Force on Quality Nurse Practitioner Education (NTF), a group of national nursing organizations, list six: family/individual across the lifespan, adult-gerontology (primary care or acute care), pediatrics (primary care or acute care), neonatal, women's health/gender related, and psychiatric-mental health across the lifespan ([NTF Standards, 6th edition](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.nonpf.org/resource/resmgr/2022/ntfs_/ntfs_final.pdf), 2022).

| Population focus | NP track | Certifying boards (credential) |
|---|---|---|
| Family/individual across the lifespan | FNP | AANPCB (NP-C) or ANCC (FNP-BC) |
| Adult-gerontology primary care | AGPCNP | ANCC (AGPCNP-BC) or AANPCB |
| Adult-gerontology acute care | AGACNP | ANCC (AGACNP-BC) or the American Association of Critical-Care Nurses (ACNPC-AG) |
| Pediatrics, primary or acute care | PNP | PNCB (CPNP-PC or CPNP-AC) |
| Psychiatric-mental health | PMHNP | ANCC (PMHNP-BC) or AANPCB (PMHNP-C) |
| Women's health/gender related | WHNP | NCC (WHNP-BC) |
| Neonatal | NNP | NCC (NNP-BC) |

Dermatology, cardiology and oncology are practice areas, not population foci, so an NP headed for one of them still certifies in one of the six. Choose deliberately: adding a focus later means more coursework and clinical hours, typically through a post-graduate certificate. Compare three tracks side by side in [FNP vs PMHNP vs AGACNP](/blog/fnp-vs-pmhnp-vs-agacnp), or use the [specialty finder](/tools/specialty-finder) to match a focus to the work you want.

## Step 5: Finish an accredited MSN or DNP program

Certifying boards check your program, so accreditation matters before you enroll. The American Nurses Credentialing Center (ANCC) requires an FNP "master's, post-graduate certificate, or DNP" from a program accredited by the Commission on Collegiate Nursing Education (CCNE), the Accreditation Commission for Education in Nursing (ACEN) or the National League for Nursing Commission for Nursing Education Accreditation (NLN CNEA) ([ANCC: FNP certification](https://www.nursingworld.org/our-certifications/family-nurse-practitioner/), accessed November 23, 2026). The American Academy of Nurse Practitioners Certification Board (AANPCB), which does business as the Nurse Practitioners Certification Board (NPCB), requires a "graduate, postgraduate, or doctoral" FNP program accredited by a nursing accreditor recognized by the U.S. Department of Education or the Council for Higher Education Accreditation ([NPCB FNP Candidate Handbook](https://www.aanpcert.org/wp-content/uploads/FNP-Candidate-Handbook-06-04-2026.pdf), updated June 2026). Both also require three graduate courses known as the APRN core: advanced pathophysiology, advanced health assessment and advanced pharmacology.

Programs also differ in clinical placement support. Ask whether the school finds your preceptors or expects you to, and read the [preceptor guide](/blog/np-preceptor-guide) before you sign an enrollment agreement.

### How many clinical hours do NP programs require?

Three kinds of rules apply, and they come from different bodies: AACN's education framework, the NTF's standards for NP programs, and each certifying board's eligibility minimum.

| Body | Applies to | Minimum hours | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| AACN Essentials | All advanced-level nursing education, including MSN, DNP and post-graduate certificates | 500 practice hours after entry-level education | Approved April 2021, updated April 2026 |
| NTF Standards | Each NP population focused track | 750 direct patient care clinical hours, simulation excluded | Issued February 2022, phased transition |
| ANCC | FNP exam eligibility | 500 faculty-supervised clinical hours | Current FNP certification page |
| AANPCB (NPCB) | FNP exam eligibility | 500 faculty-supervised direct patient care clinical hours | Handbook updated June 2026 |
| NCC | NNP and WHNP exam eligibility | 750 direct patient care hours | Applicants graduating after December 31, 2029 |

AACN's Essentials call for "a minimum of 500 practice hours in the discipline of nursing, post entry-level education," and note that specialty and certifying bodies add their own time-based requirements ([AACN Essentials](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/0/PDFs/Publications/Essentials-2021.pdf), updated April 2026). The NTF standard, Criterion III.H, sets the 750 hour minimum per track and says "Simulation is not direct patient care" ([NTF Standards](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.nonpf.org/resource/resmgr/2022/ntfs_/ntfs_final.pdf)); an NTF FAQ dated June 16, 2022 expected programs to need 3 to 5 years to transition, "with some outliers" ([NTF FAQ](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.nonpf.org/resource/resmgr/2022/ntfs_/20220616_ntfs_faq_.pdf)), so ask each program whether its curriculum already meets it. The National Certification Corporation (NCC) also says that from January 1, 2029, NPs must test within five years of graduating ([NCC](https://www.nccwebsite.org/faculty-students-state-boards-volunteers/750-direct-patient-care-clinical-hours-and-5)).

The 1,000 hour DNP figure comes from AACN's 2006 DNP Essentials, which said programs "should provide a minimum of 1,000 hours of practice post-baccalaureate as part of a supervised academic program" ([AACN DNP Essentials](https://www.aacnnursing.org/Portals/0/PDFs/Publications/DNPEssentials.pdf)). AACN's current Essentials, which in 2021 succeeded its separate baccalaureate, master's and doctoral documents, set no DNP-specific hour count beyond the 500 hour floor, so ask each DNP program how many practice hours its plan of study includes.

### Do nurse practitioners need a doctorate?

Not for certification: neither FNP certifying board requires one, and BLS says NPs "typically need at least a master's degree in an advanced practice nursing field" ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm)). In 2018 the National Organization of Nurse Practitioner Faculties (NONPF) called for moving entry-level NP preparation to the DNP by 2025, and it reaffirmed that position in April 2023 ([AACN DNP fact sheet](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/dnp-fact-sheet), updated May 2026). That was a faculty organization's goal, not a licensing rule. State boards set their own APRN education rules, so confirm yours through the [licensure checker](/tools/licensure-checker).

## Step 6: Pass a national certification exam

Certification is the national board exam for your population focus; the table in Step 4 shows which boards certify each one. For family nurse practitioners there are two:

- **AANPCB, operating as NPCB.** Its FNP exam has 150 multiple-choice questions, 135 of them scored, with 3 hours to finish, and its handbook calls its registered certification mark NP-C. MSN and post-graduate certificate applicants may apply up to 6 months before program completion and DNP applicants up to 12 months before; you can test once all coursework and clinical hours are complete, even if your degree is conferred later ([NPCB FNP Candidate Handbook](https://www.aanpcert.org/wp-content/uploads/FNP-Candidate-Handbook-06-04-2026.pdf)).
- **ANCC.** Its FNP exam has 175 questions, 150 of them scored, with 3.5 hours to finish, and it awards the FNP-BC ([ANCC: FNP certification](https://www.nursingworld.org/our-certifications/family-nurse-practitioner/)). A new FNP test content outline took effect October 30, 2026 ([ANCC FNP Test Content Outline](https://www.nursingworld.org/globalassets/study-aids/family-nurse-practitioner-tco_04062026.pdf)).

Our [AANP vs ANCC comparison](/blog/fnp-certification-aanp-vs-ancc) sets the two FNP exams side by side, and the [certification overview](/blog/np-certification-overview) maps every other focus to its board.

## Step 7: Get your state APRN license

Certification alone does not let you practice. Your state board of nursing issues the APRN license or recognition, and its application, practice and supervision rules are its own. Start with the [licensure checker](/tools/licensure-checker) for your board's requirements, and the [scope of practice explorer](/scope-of-practice) for its practice rules.

Practice authority varies as well. The [AANP State Practice Environment map](https://www.aanp.org/advocacy/state/state-practice-environment) classifies 27 states + DC as full practice (AANP, 2026), although several of those states first require a transition-to-practice period of collaborative or supervised practice. The [full practice authority guide](/resources/fpa-guide) explains what the classification changes.

## Step 8: Register with the DEA and get an NPI

- **DEA registration.** To prescribe controlled substances you register with the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) as a mid-level practitioner, a category that includes nurse practitioners. The DEA requires "authority to dispense controlled substances under the laws of the state" first, and a separate registration in each state where you practice ([DEA registration Q&A](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/faq/registration-faq.html)).
- **State prescribing rules.** Prescriptive authority itself comes from state law, so ask your board of nursing whether your state requires a separate prescribing, furnishing or controlled substance registration before you apply to the DEA. Our guide to [whether NPs can prescribe controlled substances](/blog/can-nurse-practitioners-prescribe-controlled-substances) covers the federal side.
- **NPI.** A National Provider Identifier is "a unique 10-digit number used to identify health care providers," assigned through the CMS system called NPPES, and HIPAA-covered providers must obtain one ([CMS: NPIs](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/administrative-simplification/unique-identifiers/npis), updated March 16, 2026).
- **Credentialing.** Your employer then verifies all of it at the source. The [credentialing checklist](/blog/np-credentialing-checklist) lists the documents to assemble once.

## What does it cost to become a nurse practitioner?

There is no honest single figure. Tuition is set by each school and changes with the degree, the program and full-time or part-time study, and certification, licensure and DEA fees are set by the boards and agencies that charge them. Ask each program for its full cost of attendance, and check each board's current fee page before you budget; the [AANP vs ANCC comparison](/blog/fnp-certification-aanp-vs-ancc) links both FNP boards' fee pages.

Federal borrowing for graduate school also changed on July 1, 2026. Under the Department of Education's rule, "Beginning on July 1, 2026, a graduate student or professional student may not borrow a Direct PLUS Loan" ([34 CFR 685.200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-VI/part-685/subpart-B/section-685.200)). The exception covers students enrolled in a program as of June 30, 2026 who received a Direct Loan for that program before July 1, 2026, during their expected time to credential. Federal Student Aid lists annual Direct Unsubsidized limits of $20,500 for graduate students and $50,000 for professional students, with aggregate limits of $100,000 and $200,000 ([FSA: Loan Limits FAQ](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf), May 20, 2026).

Which limit applies to NP students has been in litigation. In its update of July 10, 2026, the Department said that during a court stay it treats MSN, DNP and nurse anesthesia (DNAP) programs as professional degree programs, and warned that the classification may change with the litigation ([FSA electronic announcement GENERAL-26-42](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-06-29/update-list-professional-degree-programs-due-court-order-updated-july-10-2026)). Our [student loan limits guide](/blog/np-student-loan-limits-professional-degree) tracks the open cases; your financial aid office and [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) decide your own award.

## What do nurse practitioners earn, and is the field growing?

The median annual wage for nurse practitioners was $132,300 in May 2025, with the 10th percentile at $101,340 and the 90th percentile at $174,420 (BLS OEWS, May 2025). BLS Employment Projections put nurse practitioner employment growth at about 41% from 2025 to 2035, the fastest projected growth of any detailed occupation in that release. Both are national figures, not a quote for your town or specialty; the [salary guide](/salary-guide) tracks pay and the state medians it can support from live postings.

## Find your first NP job

Start searching in your final year of school, because hiring, licensure and credentialing can run in parallel.

1. **Decide whether you want structured onboarding.** Fellowships and residencies offer supervised training in the first year of practice; compare them in our [fellowship and residency guide](/blog/np-fellowship-residency-programs).
2. **Search roles that hire new graduates.** The [new grad NP jobs](/jobs/new-grad) page collects postings open to recent graduates, and the [first NP job guide](/blog/new-grad-np-first-job) covers how to land one.
3. **Set an alert.** [Job alerts](/job-alerts) email you new postings that match your search while you finish school and boards.
4. **Ask about the start date.** Ask every employer how long its credentialing usually takes and what happens to your start date if your certification or license arrives late.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to become a nurse practitioner?

It depends on your starting point. Counting a BSN that typically takes 4 years (BLS), a BSN-to-DNP path is about seven to eight years of school under AACN's DNP length guidance. With a bachelor's degree in another field, a direct-entry master's generally takes about 2 to 3 years, and AACN puts RN-to-MSN bridges for associate degree and diploma RNs at up to 2 to 3 years. Certification, licensure and any RN experience your program requires add time.

### How many years of school does it take to become an NP?

Count a four-year BSN plus your graduate program. AACN's DNP guidance planned three calendar years of full-time study after the BSN, or four years on a traditional academic calendar, so the doctoral route is about seven to eight years of school. Each school sets the length of its MSN, so ask for the plan of study and expected completion date before you enroll.

### Can you become a nurse practitioner without a BSN?

Yes, but not without an RN license. RNs with an associate degree or diploma can take an RN-to-MSN bridge, which AACN says generally takes up to 2 to 3 years, and people with a bachelor's degree in another field can take an accelerated or entry-level master's, which AACN says takes about 2 to 3 years. Confirm that the program prepares you for an NP population focus and national certification.

### What are the requirements to become a nurse practitioner?

BLS lists four: a registered nursing license, an accredited graduate-level program, a passing result on a national certification exam and an APRN license. Within those, your program sets admission rules such as RN experience, your certifying board sets coursework and clinical hour minimums, and your state board of nursing sets licensure and practice rules.

### How many clinical hours do NP programs require?

The 2022 NTF standard calls for at least 750 direct patient care clinical hours per NP population focused track, with simulation excluded, and AACN's Essentials set a 500 practice hour floor for advanced nursing education. For FNP certification, ANCC and AANPCB each require at least 500 faculty-supervised clinical hours, and NCC will require 750 for NNP and WHNP applicants graduating after December 31, 2029.

### Do you need a DNP to become a nurse practitioner?

Not under the certification rules we checked in November 2026. ANCC accepts an FNP master's, post-graduate certificate or DNP, and AANPCB accepts graduate, postgraduate or doctoral FNP programs. NONPF set a goal in 2018 of moving entry-level NP education to the DNP by 2025, but that was a faculty organization's goal, not a licensing rule. Confirm your state board's education rule.

### What certification does a nurse practitioner need?

You need national certification in the population focus your program prepared you for. Family nurse practitioners can certify through AANPCB (operating as NPCB), whose handbook names its mark NP-C, or through ANCC, which awards the FNP-BC. Pediatric NPs certify through PNCB, neonatal and women's health NPs through NCC, and other foci have their own boards.
