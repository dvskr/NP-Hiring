## The new-grad market is better than it feels

Job hunting without NP experience feels like a closed loop — postings ask for experience you can only get from a job. But the macro picture is firmly on your side: BLS projects about 40% employment growth for nurse practitioners from 2024 to 2034, and 90 million+ Americans live in federally designated primary-care Health Professional Shortage Areas (HRSA, 2025). Employers in shortage areas can't fill seats with experienced NPs alone; many have built onboarding programs specifically to grow new grads.

Start your search where those programs live: [new-grad NP listings](/jobs/new-grad), community health centers, and FQHCs.

## What a good first job actually looks like

Evaluate offers on support structure first, salary second. The markers that separate a launchpad from a burnout factory:

- **A named preceptor or mentor** — not "collaborative team environment," but a specific clinician with time carved out for you.
- **A gradual caseload ramp** — new-grad-friendly employers typically ramp schedules over the first 3–6 months rather than assigning a full panel on day one.
- **Protected documentation and supervision time** in your early months.
- **A realistic patient-per-day expectation** for year one, stated out loud in the interview.

Ask directly: "What does my first 90 days look like? Who reviews my charts? When does my schedule reach full volume?" Employers with real programs answer specifically; the vague ones just told you something important too.

## What new-grad NP roles pay

New-grad positions on this board typically post at $95K–$120K, against a national all-NP median of $129,210 (BLS OEWS, May 2024). Location and setting move you within that band more than negotiation will in year one.

One lever routinely worth more than base salary: **loan repayment**. Community health centers and FQHCs in federally designated shortage areas broadly qualify for National Health Service Corps loan repayment, and the award can outweigh a base-pay difference several times over. Award amounts scale with the site's shortage score and your service commitment, so check the current terms at [nhsc.hrsa.gov](https://nhsc.hrsa.gov/loan-repayment) and ask the employer whether the site is eligible. Our [salary guide](/salary-guide) shows live state-level averages so you can calibrate an offer against the local market, not a national headline.

## Making yourself hireable without NP experience

**Lead with your RN history.** Years at the bedside, charge experience, and the patient populations you know are your differentiation — a new-grad NP with ICU years is not "inexperienced" to an acute-care employer.

**Use your clinical rotations.** Rotation sites are your warmest leads; preceptors who saw your work are your strongest references. Tell every preceptor you're job hunting before you graduate.

**Get licensure moving early.** Certification and state licensure timelines gate your start date, and employers weigh time-to-start. Begin your state application as soon as you're eligible — our [state licensure guides](/resources) map the sequence for all 51 jurisdictions.

**Apply beyond the "new grad" label.** Many postings that don't say new-grad will consider one with the right RN background. A tailored cover letter naming your rotation experience in their setting beats a generic application to a "new grad" posting.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do new grad NPs make?

New-grad NP roles on this board typically start at $95K–$120K, compared with a $129,210 national median for all nurse practitioners (BLS OEWS, May 2024). Setting and state drive most of the variation.

### Where is it easiest to get hired as a new grad NP?

Community health centers, FQHCs, hospital new-grad fellowship programs, and large group practices hire new grads most reliably — especially in federally designated shortage areas, where demand structurally exceeds the supply of experienced NPs.

### Should I take the highest-paying first job?

Usually not on salary alone. Structured onboarding — a named preceptor, gradual caseload ramp, protected supervision time — compounds into faster skill and earnings growth than a modest base difference. Loan-repayment eligibility at National Health Service Corps sites can also outweigh base-pay gaps; check current award terms at nhsc.hrsa.gov.

### How long does it take to find a first NP job?

It varies with market, specialty, and licensure timing — the biggest controllable factor. Starting your certification and state licensure paperwork immediately upon eligibility shortens your time-to-offer more than any resume tweak.

### Do employers care about my RN experience?

Strongly. Your bedside years, the acuity you handled, and the populations you served are exactly what distinguishes you from other new grads — put them at the top of the resume, not the bottom.
