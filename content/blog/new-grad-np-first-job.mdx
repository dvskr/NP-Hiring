## The new-grad market is stronger than it feels

Job hunting without NP experience can feel like a closed loop: postings ask for experience you can only gain from a job. The long-run picture still favors you. BLS projects about 41% employment growth for nurse practitioners from 2025 to 2035, and 100 million+ Americans live in federally designated primary-care Health Professional Shortage Areas (HRSA, 2026). Employers in shortage areas cannot fill positions with experienced NPs alone, and many have built onboarding programs specifically to develop new graduates.

Be realistic about the short run, though. A national projection is not a forecast for your city or specialty, and only some postings explicitly welcome new graduates (the [State of NP Hiring report](/reports/state-of-np-hiring-2026) shows the current share on NP Hiring). Our [NP job outlook](/blog/nurse-practitioner-job-outlook) covers the market.

## Where do new grad NP jobs come from?

New grad roles come mainly from employers that train NPs rather than wait for experienced ones:

- **Fellowships and residencies.** A postgraduate NP fellowship or residency is a structured first year of supervised practice plus teaching. The [VA Long Beach primary care NP residency](https://www.va.gov/long-beach-health-care/work-with-us/internships-and-fellowships/primary-care-nurse-practitioner-residency-program/), funded by the VA Office of Academic Affiliations, is a 12-month paid program for graduates within 12 months of finishing, with applications from March 15 to April 1 (page updated July 10, 2026). ANCC accredits APRN and PA transition programs through [APPFA](https://www.nursingworld.org/organizational-programs/accreditation/appfa/). See our [guide to NP fellowship and residency programs](/blog/np-fellowship-residency-programs).
- **Community health centers and FQHCs.** Shortage-area clinics hire new grads most reliably. Browse [community health NP jobs](/jobs/community-health).
- **Health systems and large group practices** with a written onboarding plan.
- **Your rotation sites.** Preceptors who saw your work are your warmest leads and strongest references.

## What a good first job looks like

Evaluate offers on support structure first and salary second. Look for:

- **A named preceptor or mentor.** Not a "collaborative team environment," but a specific clinician with time set aside for you.
- **A gradual caseload ramp** over the first months, not a full panel on day one.
- **Protected documentation and supervision time** during your early months.
- **A realistic patient-per-day expectation** for year one.

Ask directly: "What do my first 90 days look like? Who reviews my charts? When does my schedule reach full volume?" Employers with established programs answer specifically.

## What new-grad NP roles pay

New-grad positions sit below the national median of $132,300 for all nurse practitioners (BLS OEWS, May 2025), because that median covers NPs at every experience level. Location and setting will move your first offer more than negotiation will. Where this board publishes a state median (once enough postings with disclosed pay from enough employers support it), the [salary guide](/salary-guide) shows it.

One lever often worth more than base salary is **loan repayment**. Many community health centers and FQHCs in federally designated shortage areas are National Health Service Corps sites, and award terms depend on the site and your service commitment, so check the current terms at [nhsc.hrsa.gov](https://nhsc.hrsa.gov/loan-repayment) and ask the employer whether the site is eligible.

## How to apply as a new grad NP

Work these steps in your final semester, not after graduation:

1. **Start licensure early.** Certification and state licensure timelines set your start date, and employers weigh time-to-start. Our [state licensure guides](/resources) map the sequence for all 51 jurisdictions.
2. **Check fellowship deadlines.** Some close before you finish school; the VA Long Beach window above closes April 1.
3. **Lead your resume with RN history.** A new-grad NP with ICU years is not "inexperienced" to an acute-care employer. Our [NP resume guide](/blog/np-resume-guide) shows the format.
4. **Tell every preceptor you are job hunting** and ask who will serve as a reference.
5. **Tailor each cover letter** to the setting, naming your rotation experience there. See the [NP cover letter guide](/blog/np-cover-letter-guide).
6. **Apply beyond the "new grad" label.** Many postings that do not mention new grads will still consider one with the right RN background.
7. **Prepare for supervision questions** with our [NP interview questions](/blog/np-interview-questions), and bring your own questions about onboarding.

Then watch [new grad NP jobs](/jobs/new-grad) and set a [job alert](/job-alerts) so new postings reach you first.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do new grad NPs find their first job?

Start with employers that train new NPs: fellowship and residency programs, community health centers and FQHCs, VA medical centers, large group practices, and your clinical rotation sites, where preceptors know your work. Begin licensure paperwork early, tailor each application, and apply to postings that do not say "new grad" when your RN background fits the setting.

### Should a new grad NP do a residency or fellowship?

It is worth considering if you want a structured first year, a new specialty or a setting more acute than your rotations. A program adds supervised practice and teaching; it does not replace certification or state licensure. Compare program length, teaching time, service commitments and pay against direct-hire offers that include a strong preceptorship.

### How much do new grad NPs make?

Below the $132,300 national median for all nurse practitioners (BLS OEWS, May 2025), which covers every experience level. Setting and state drive most of the variation, so check the state median in the salary guide wherever this board publishes one, and compare the posted pay in live listings.

### Should I take the highest-paying first job?

Usually not on salary alone. Structured onboarding (a named preceptor, a gradual caseload ramp, and protected supervision time) compounds into faster skill development and better offers later than a modest base-pay difference. Loan-repayment eligibility at National Health Service Corps sites can also outweigh base-pay gaps; check current award terms at nhsc.hrsa.gov.

### How long does it take to find a first NP job?

It varies with the market, your specialty, and licensure timing, which is the biggest controllable factor. Starting your certification and state licensure paperwork as soon as you are eligible shortens your time-to-offer more than any resume adjustment.
